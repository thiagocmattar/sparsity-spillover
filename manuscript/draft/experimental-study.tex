\section{Experimental study}
\label{sec:experiments}

We pretrain Pythia-14M, 70M, and 410M from random initialization~\citep{biderman2023pythia} on MiniPile~\citep{kaddour2023minipile}. Each run receives 712 optimizer steps and 1.493 billion input tokens. Within each size, runs share initialization, data order and training budget. We evaluate final-checkpoint validation loss and $\Smodel$ on the full validation set. Table~\ref{tab:interventions} lists the intervention recipes; Appendix~\ref{app:training-protocol} gives the full protocol.

\textbf{Roles of the three model sizes.}
The 14M study provides controlled intervention comparisons; 70M tests whether the observed sparse structures remain exploitable after adapting the implementation. We retain 410M as a fixed-token sparsity stress test, rather than a quality-scaling result. Its base-model loss exceeds 70M's under a smaller token-to-parameter ratio and a lower learning rate; these effects are not isolated. Appendix~\ref{app:410m-results} reports all 410M endpoints and clipping curves, and Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization} shows the training trajectories. The detailed intervention and runtime analyses therefore focus on 14M and 70M.

For comparison, we apply TEAL-style post-hoc magnitude thresholding~\citep{liu2024teal} at $a,m,h,z$, using percentile targets $p=0,0.1,\ldots,0.9$. Site--layer thresholds are calibrated separately on ten training blocks. Figures~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview} and~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency} show the quality--sparsity trade-offs and full-model latencies at 14M and 70M, respectively.

\textbf{Interventions extend sparsity at a quality cost.}
At $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $27.48\%$ and $40.60\%$ model-wide sparsity at 14M and 70M: $91.8\%$ and $82.2\%$ of the respective ceilings. Validation loss rises by $0.62$ and $1.12$ relative to each size's base model. At moderate thresholds ($\kappa\leq0.1$), pressure confined to $h$ gives lower loss than global pressure at similar sparsity; Section~\ref{sec:paired-results} examines the matched comparisons.

\textbf{The 14M kernel does not transfer efficiently to 70M.}
The direct adaptation of the 14M kernel to 70M can exploit the additional sparsity to reduce inference latency, but its overhead largely offsets these gains. In the matched sweep, the original port takes 3.325\,ms on the base model, compared with 1.611\,ms for native PyTorch, and its best sparse recipe reaches only $1.013\times$ the native base-model speed. Additional optimization reduces repeated sparsity checks at $h,z$, uses a scalar path for very sparse rows, and improves dense projections, attention and the output head. On $T_7/P_h$ at $\kappa=0.5$, this reduces latency from 1.590 to 1.088\,ms ($1.481\times$ the native base-model speed). All 26 checkpoints pass full-validation numerical checks in three fresh processes (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-transfer}). The lower-loss $T_2/P_h$ points, however, remain slower than native: loss is only $0.029$--$0.093$ above Base, but optimized latency is 1.956--2.504\,ms. Thus, the favorable 14M quality--latency balance does not transfer directly.
