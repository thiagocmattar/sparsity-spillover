\begin{abstract}
Activation zeros, zero-operand computation, and inference latency can rank
sparsification recipes differently. We study these disagreements in 54
Pythia pretraining conditions on MiniPile, varying activation pressure,
thresholding, and site placement with matched initialization and data order
within each size. Model-wide sparsity measures the fraction of counted scalar
multiplications with a zero activation operand; selected-site reach accounts for architectural and
inference-workload differences. Pressure's marginal benefit depends on both
threshold and target set. At the largest tested threshold, seven-site
thresholding with orthogonal L1 (OL1) pressure
reaches 27.48\%, 40.60\%, and 80.62\% model-wide sparsity at 14M, 70M, and
410M, with dense-relative validation-loss penalties of 0.621, 1.116, and
0.573, respectively. These are 91.8\%, 82.2\%, and 92.4\% of selected-site
reach. At that 14M
endpoint, expanding to seven sites reduces feed-forward (FFN) up-input sparsity while increasing
attention sparsity. A specialized kernel profitably exploits projection
zero fragments, but attention skipping slows every tested checkpoint.
Seven-site's larger native-relative speedup also coexists with higher
absolute optimized latency because its native reference is slower.
These single-seed comparisons motivate evaluating quality, computational
reach, and execution structure together.
\end{abstract}
