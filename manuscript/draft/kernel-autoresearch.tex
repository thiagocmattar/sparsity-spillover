\subsection{When model-wide sparsity translates to speedup}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}
\label{sec:kernel-autoresearch}

\textbf{The additional high-threshold sparsity is concentrated in $QK/PV$ products.}
Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping} decomposes $\Smodel$ by operation at 14M.
At $\kappa=0.5$, extending $T_7$ pressure from $P_h$ to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$
raises the $QK/PV$ contribution from $6.38$ to $16.77$ percentage points,
accounting for most of the $10.82$-point increase in model-wide sparsity.
The attention contribution is much smaller at $\kappa=0.05$.

\textbf{More zero products need not mean lower latency.}
GPUs execute groups of products, and detecting zeros also costs time.
Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-autoresearch} shows the matrix instructions actually
bypassed. For FFN-down ($h$) and attention-output ($z$), our kernel detects
empty groups before reading the corresponding weights and handles nearly
empty rows using their surviving values. This can avoid both arithmetic
and data movement. Matched 14M controls locate the skipping benefit in the
projection path; they do not separate the individual $h/z$ time savings.

The $QK/PV$ path instead tests already-loaded operands and retains attention
normalization and output work. A zero score is not a zero attention
probability. In all 35 matched 14M controls, enabling attention skipping
increases latency with projection skipping held fixed, despite substantial
$QK/PV$ bypass in some settings. The checks and remaining work can outweigh
the saved arithmetic. This limits the tested implementation, not the
potential value of attention sparsity. The 70M bars show instruction bypass
without isolating its timing benefit; native-relative speedup also includes
fusion (Appendices~\ref{app:kernel-results} and~\ref{app:kernel-diagnostics}).
