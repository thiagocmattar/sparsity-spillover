\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Activation sparsity can reduce inference cost when specialized kernels exploit
zero activations~\citep{liu2024teal,cetin2026sparser}. Choosing how to create
those zeros requires more than a local sparsity percentage: their sites
determine the affected multiplication work, and skipping that work adds
overhead. We ask how pressure, thresholding and placement change the quality
cost of sparsification, and which opportunities translate into faster execution.

Existing recipes combine these choices differently: TEAL uses post-hoc
thresholding with allocation across projections; ProSparse combines ReLU,
progressive pressure and threshold shifting; Q-Sparse selects top-$k$
projection inputs~\citep{liu2024teal,song2024prosparse,wang2024qsparse}.
Their ablations motivate examining the additional value of pressure as the
threshold and sites change. We distinguish \emph{pressure}, which encourages
small magnitudes, from \emph{thresholding}, which applies specified zeroing
maps, and \emph{placement}, which selects where each acts.

We pretrain Pythia-family models from scratch for controlled comparisons
across sizes~\citep{biderman2023pythia}. At 14M, 30 conditions and 29 paired
comparisons show that pressure's marginal effect depends on the threshold.
At the largest threshold, adding orthogonal pressure to seven-site
thresholding raises model-wide sparsity from $15.39\%$ to $27.48\%$, against
$29.95\%$ architectural reach, for $+0.1265$ loss relative to the unpressured
recipe and $+0.6208$ relative to unmodified A0. This recipe has greater
model-wide sparsity than its four-site counterpart despite fewer pooled FFN
zeros; operation counts explain the reversal. Selected recipes extend to
70M and 410M, where the high-threshold seven-site advantage persists but
lower-threshold orderings vary. Uniform post-hoc clipping provides a
supporting comparison: small quality budgets can favor clipped endpoints.

A human-guided coding-agent case study then measures execution on one
RTX5090, using BF16, batch-one, uncached 2,048-token inference. The selected
implementation is $1.234\times$ faster than native PyTorch/SDPA geometrically
across all 30 14M checkpoints. Sparse paths add $1.043\times$ over matched
fusion with skipping disabled, helping 14 checkpoints; disabling attention
skipping improves every checkpoint. These comparisons connect an
intervention's quality cost and affected operations to the instruction
granularity and overhead that determine whether its zeros are useful.
