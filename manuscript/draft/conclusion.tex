\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Pressure's additional value depends on the threshold and target objective:
it can improve both observed axes at one setting and exchange quality for
zeros at another. Broader attention intervention can also raise model-wide
sparsity while reducing pooled FFN sparsity. Recipe comparisons therefore
require paired quality changes and operation-weighted counts. Selecting a
model under a loss budget and selecting its execution kernel require further
comparisons: neither a high sparsity percentage nor a same-checkpoint
native-relative speedup establishes a quality-matched model advantage.

Architectural reach, observed zero products, skipped instructions and runtime
are distinct quantities. Here, fusion and sparse paths both contribute, but
attention skipping loses despite bypassing many instructions. These findings
concern one-seed MiniPile pretraining, selected larger-size recipes, a limited
uniform-clipping reference and one 14M kernel workload, with FP16 counts and
BF16 timing. The next questions are whether the pressure relationships
replicate with existing coefficients held fixed, and whether the execution
trade-off persists at larger widths.
