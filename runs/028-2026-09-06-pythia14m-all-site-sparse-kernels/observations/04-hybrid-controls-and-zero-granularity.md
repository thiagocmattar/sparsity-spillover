# Why specialization helps, but does not make all R_model opportunity free

## Question, method, and coverage

Did the original implementation discard sparse variants, or fail to exploit
their sparse operands? Which changes improve speed, and why does sparse
attention remain a negative contribution? Full study identity, numerical
qualification, timing design and 500-document/338-block coverage are in
[observation 03](03-hybrid-rmodel-speedup-and-sparsity.md).

The original K19+K18 implementation exploited hW2 and zWo, not aQKV, mW1,
QK and PV. The checkpoint variants did not discard those zeros. K050 now
provides sparsity-aware paths for all six block matmul families, uniformly
across the cohort. The LM head stays dense. No new pruning, 2:4 constraint,
gate change or released-weight initialization is introduced.

K050 combines wider input-projection tiles with a short-row h/z hybrid:
rows with at most two range-safe nonzeros use SIMT products; other rows use
MMA with a support mask to jump over empty K tiles. Padded hardware rows,
support detection, operand checks and memory movement still cost time.
Attention uses conditional ordinary BF16 MMA. The six block families have
different shapes and zero patterns, so one generic scalar-zero rule is not
an efficient implementation for all of them.

The new paired LayerNorm reuses a reduction over GPT-NeoX's shared residual
input for the parallel attention and MLP branches, retains their distinct
affine parameters, and fuses the existing a/m gates after BF16 rounding.
This is an overhead/fusion optimization, not skipped logical products.
The unfused K049 control retains the same sparse matmuls to isolate its effect.
The sparse-path and attention controls retain the K050 normalization fusion.

Diagnostics are `../artifacts/final-cNN-p1-002/diagnostics.json`, generated by
frozen `../115_hybrid_diagnostics.py`. All seven sites at all six layers are
covered on all 338 blocks: exact/near zeros, RMS/L2, occupancy histograms,
weight statistics, scalar logical counts, hybrid counters and instrumented
attention branches. Hybrid MMA and SIMT counts are independently checked
against actual operands and pooled as integers before division. These are
kernel dispatch counts, not hardware-profiler instruction counts or memory
traffic measurements. The scalar lower bound excludes weight zeros and PV
probability-underflow zeros. Attention MMA potential includes causal padding;
h/z potential has twofold M8-to-M16 row padding. Bypassed h/z MMA includes
SIMT substitution, not just zero multiplications eliminated.

## Results

At c30, unfused K049 / K050 is 1.157045 (process range 1.156539-1.157645).
Previous / K050 is 1.101296 (1.101115-1.101456). All six previous-qualified
variants, c08/c15/c20/c25/c30/c35, are faster with K050. The other previous
results remain visible but cannot establish valid speedups. Native eager/new
eager ranges 1.863319 (A0) to 2.979214 (c29); this is distinct from the stronger
graph-to-graph baseline in the main figure.

Attention skipping alone is slower for every variant. At c30 its ratio is
0.988432 (0.988087-0.988815): about 1.17% higher latency with attention skips
enabled, despite substantial MMA bypass. At c25 the ratio is 0.983134.
Checks, scheduling, softmax/reductions and operand movement can outweigh
the removed arithmetic. These controls measure the net effect, not an
exhaustive decomposition of those overheads.

The full-validation c30 pooled counters are:

| a QKV | 75.6292 | 8.4385 | not applicable |

| z Wo | 99.9170 | 99.6449 | 51,767,296 |

The h/z SIMT counts are respectively 0.069744% and 0.076074% of their logical
products. Their MMA bypass fractions use the padded potential and include
replacement by those SIMT products. They are not scalar zero percentages.

The clearest granularity counterexample is mW1: 186,859,244,544 zero scalar
products out of 272,193,552,384, yet all 132,907,008 MMA atoms execute.
Scattered zeros do not create the all-zero operand fragments this path needs.
Conversely, QK/PV bypass 58.0%/67.2% of their MMA potential but remain slower
in the matched attention control. Sparsity exists; useful grouping and net
hardware cost determine whether it becomes latency savings.

## Interpretation and limits

Yes, another level of specialization was needed, and the hybrid row paths
provide a substantial benefit at high sparsity. That does not imply every
matmul site is now profitable. The attention result is explicitly negative;
the dense LM head and remaining non-matmul work also limit full-model gains.
No scalar-zero percentage alone determines memory traffic or runtime.

A zero QK dot product is a zero **logit**, not a zero softmax probability.
A zero query yields uniform attention over its allowed causal prefix; its
output cannot simply be discarded. Exact zero-aware arithmetic therefore
cannot treat zero scores as masked attention entries. This semantic issue is
separate from the fragment-granularity and overhead issues measured here.

The broader supported conclusion is the bounded one in observation 03.
There is no claim that all R_model opportunity was converted, that higher
R_model guarantees monotonic speedup, or that the checkpoints have matched
predictive quality. Figures were rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected;
the numerical exceptions at c33/c34 are retained and documented, not hidden
behind a claim of exact equivalence. No manuscript claim is promoted.
