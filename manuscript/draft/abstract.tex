\begin{abstract}
Activation sparsity promises cheaper inference, but interventions that create zeros need not produce execution gains. We isolate pressure, thresholding and their placement through matched Pythia-14M/70M pretraining on MiniPile, connecting validation loss to model-wide activation sparsity, its architectural ceiling, and measured latency. Broad interventions approach the sparsity ceiling at a quality cost.
\textbf{Local pressure can outperform broad pressure:} at moderate thresholds, FFN-hidden pressure lowers loss in all 16 matched comparisons; across the full threshold grid, it also lowers latency in 18 of 20. It also increases sparsity at unpressured attention outputs.
\textbf{Execution gains depend on where zeros occur:} in a 14M kernel ablation, skipping FFN-down and attention-output projections reduces latency, whereas joint attention-product skipping increases it. These one-seed results and batch-one RTX5090 measurements support co-designing training interventions and kernels to produce exploitable zero structure.
\end{abstract}
