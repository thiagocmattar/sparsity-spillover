\section{Conclusion}
Matched interventions show that the location of gates and magnitude pressure
substantially changes the quality--sparsity trade-off in transformer
pretraining. Attention-operand gating opens opportunity beyond the
feed-forward and projection inputs, while broader pressure alone can
increase the quality cost. The 14M study and selected 70M results establish
a quality-dependent extension of the evaluated uniform-clipping frontier.
Operation-level accounting and a negative execution test identify the
next requirement: learning sparsity patterns whose structure can support
efficient inference.
