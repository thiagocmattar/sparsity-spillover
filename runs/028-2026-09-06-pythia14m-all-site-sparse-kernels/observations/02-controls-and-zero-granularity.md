# Why all-site zero skipping does not capture all logical opportunity

## Question and sources

Does K036 improve on the previous kernel under matched execution, does
attention skipping itself save time, and do scalar zero rates translate into
empty tensor-core fragments? The complete study, frozen identity, timing
method, seed, quality gate, and500-document/338-block coverage are specified
in [observation01](01-rmodel-speedup-and-skip-contribution.md).

Additional raw evidence is `../artifacts/final-cNN-p1-001/diagnostics.json` for
all35 variants, generated by frozen `../58_study_diagnostics.py`. Diagnostics
cover all338 validation blocks once per checkpoint: all seven activation
sites at all six layers, exact/near zeros, RMS/L2 statistics, row occupancy,
weight statistics, logical counts, projection fragment counts and instrumented
attention branch counts. The relevant c30 case is
`../artifacts/final-c30-p1-001/diagnostics.json`.

## Aggregation and figure caption

Counts are pooled as integers across blocks and layers before division.
Projection bypass counts are derived from the actual gated BF16 operand
fragments; attention counts are instrumented at the kernel's MMA dispatch.
These are kernel MMA-atom counts, not hardware-profiler instruction counters.
The physical attention denominator includes causal padding; scalar counts use
the canonical causal logical workload. Their percentages have different
denominators and cannot be divided to estimate the fraction of R_model captured.
PV scalar counts are a V-zero-only lower bound: probability underflow zeros and
weight zeros are excluded. BF16 counters do not replace the source FP16 R_model.

## Results

K036 matched eager speedup spans1.73484x(A0) to2.23514x(c28). In matched graph
execution, c30 new latency has pooled median0.59698ms versus previous0.52158ms
and native0.82386ms. Its paired previous/new ratio is0.87371x: the new graph
remains slower than the previous graph, despite its broader coverage and
better numerical robustness. Previous qualification occurs only at
c08/c15/c20/c25/c30/c35; its largest logit discrepancy elsewhere is1.9375.
No failed checkpoint is silently replaced with native execution.

Attention skipping alone is slower for every variant. At c30 the ratio is
0.98851x, process range0.98810-0.98901; at c25 it is0.98419x,
range0.98396-0.98435. Removed MMAs do not establish an attention speed win.

The full-validation c30 counters are:

| a QKV | 75.6292 | 8.4385 |
| m W1 | 68.6494 | 0.0000 |
| h W2 | 99.8755 | 81.7932 |
| z Wo | 99.9170 | 97.3061 |
| QK | 97.2366 | 57.9952 |

Most directly, mW1 executes all132,907,008 MMA atoms despite
186,859,244,544 zero scalar products out of272,193,552,384. Its zero entries
are not arranged as all-zero16x16 A fragments. This is a granularity failure,
not discarded activation sparsity. Attention bypasses substantial fragment
work but still pays operand checks, softmax/reductions, movement and scheduling
costs. The scalar/MMA mismatch and negative attention ablation independently
limit claims about R_model translating to time.

## Interpretation and limits

The original K19+K18 implementation skipped hW2/zWo work, not aQKV,mW1,QK/PV.
The current K036 supplies fragment-level skip paths at all six. That change
does not imply arbitrary unstructured scalar zeros become free. A zero
attention dot product is also not a zero softmax probability; a zero query
has uniform causal attention and cannot simply be dropped. No extra pruning,
2:4 structural constraint, altered gates, or selective validation exclusion
has been introduced to force favorable timings.

These controls support additional implementation specialization as a possible
route, not a proven remedy or universal positive R_model-speedup law. The
remaining development attempts are separate from this frozen study. Both
positive and negative evidence remain retained. No manuscript claim is
approved or edited here. PDF rendered at150dpi and inspected; the bar legend
was moved down to remove an initial near-overlap with its x-axis label.
