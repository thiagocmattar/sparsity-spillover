\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}
\subsection{Sparsification interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}

An intervention specifies thresholding sites $\mathcal{N}$, functions $G$,
and pressure sites $\mathcal{P}$; these sets need not coincide. We use
$G_{+,\kappa}(x)=x\mathbf{1}\{x\geq\kappa\}$ and
$G_{\pm,\kappa}(x)=x\mathbf{1}\{|x|\geq\kappa\}$, with fixed
$\kappa\geq0$ within a condition. Unlike top-$k$ selection, these rules do
not require ranking activation magnitudes. Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}
specifies boundary and derivative conventions.

Figure~\ref{fig:pythia-block} locates the sites: $a,m$ are the normalized
attention and FFN branch inputs; $h$ is the FFN hidden activation; $z$ is
the attention context before output projection; $q,k,v$ are internal
attention operands, with $q,k$ after RoPE. The probabilities are $p$.
We use one-sided thresholds at $a,m,h,z$ and symmetric thresholds at $q,k,v$;
at $h$, the nonlinearity replaces GELU. \emph{One-, four-, and seven-site}
refer to site types repeated in every transformer block, not numbers of
layers: $\{h\}$, $\{a,m,h,z\}$, and $\{a,m,h,z,q,k,v\}$.

The pressure objective $\mathcal{L}_1$ equally averages mean absolute
activations across targeted site--layer tensors after thresholding.
\emph{Naive L1} minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_1$,
adding a weight that affects quality and sparsity~\citep{song2024prosparse,cetin2026sparser}.
We propose \emph{orthogonal L1} (OL1), inspired by gradient-conflict
projection~\citep{yu2020pcgrad,hsieh2024bloop}: it removes the pressure
component opposing the AdamW-preconditioned task direction, then caps the
correction norm relative to that direction. Ignoring stabilizers, the capped
correction maximizes alignment with the pressure direction within this norm
budget and non-opposition constraint. Once the cap binds, increasing
$\lambda$ leaves the ideal correction unchanged at a fixed optimizer state.
We fix multisite $\lambda=b=1$ for tractability and measure cap activity;
saturation is not universal. This construction does not guarantee task-loss
preservation (Appendix~\ref{app:pressure}).

\subsection{Sparsity and selected-site computational reach}
\label{sec:measurement}

\emph{Activation sparsity} is the exact-zero fraction at a site.
\emph{Model-wide sparsity} is $\Smodel=N_0/N_{\mathrm{model}}$, where $N_0$
counts scalar multiplications with a zero activation operand in block matrix
multiplications. The denominator includes those products and the dense
final vocabulary projection at every token. Counts pool layers and
validation sequences, excluding future-masked attention pairs. Softmax,
normalization, data movement and other non-matrix operations are excluded;
$\Smodel$ is a logical opportunity, not saved time (Appendix~\ref{app:accounting}).

The \emph{selected-site reach ceiling}
$\Smax=N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})/N_{\mathrm{model}}$ measures coverage
when all selected sites are entirely zero. It depends on architecture,
sequence length and output scope. Natural zeros outside this reach can make
$\Smodel>\Smax$, so $\Smodel/\Smax$ is a reference ratio, not necessarily a
bounded utilization. Table~\ref{tab:interventions} reports reach for the
declared full-sequence inference setting; Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling}
defines the counters.
