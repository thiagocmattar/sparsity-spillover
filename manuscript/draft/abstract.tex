\begin{abstract}
Activation sparsification requires choosing how to induce zeros, which
operations to target, and when exploiting them improves execution.
We study pressure, fixed thresholding and site placement through 54 Pythia
pretraining conditions on MiniPile: extensive 14M comparisons and selected
recipes at 70M and 410M, with one seed per condition.
Architectural reach quantifies the multiplication workload structurally
affected before training; model-wide sparsity measures observed zero-operand
products using the same full-model denominator.
Pressure's marginal effect depends on threshold and targets: at 14M and
$\kappa=0.5$, adding OL1 to seven-site thresholding raises sparsity from
$15.39\%$ to $27.48\%$ against $29.95\%$ reach, for $+0.1265$ loss.
This seven-site pressured recipe outperforms its four-site counterpart on
both quality and sparsity at every tested size, while lower-threshold
orderings vary.
Activation distributions and operation counts explain why the broader
recipe can yield more model-wide sparsity despite fewer FFN zeros.
On an RTX5090, specialized BF16 full-sequence inference gains
$1.234\times$ over native execution across all 30 14M checkpoints, while
sparse paths add $1.043\times$ over matched fusion and attention skipping
slows every checkpoint.
These comparisons show why intervention choice should combine paired
quality cost, architectural reach and execution granularity.
\end{abstract}
