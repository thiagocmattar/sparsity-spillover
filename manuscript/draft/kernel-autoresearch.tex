\subsection{When model-wide sparsity translates to speedup}
\label{sec:kernel-autoresearch}

$\Smodel$ measures matrix-product work exposed to zero operands, but does
not by itself guarantee lower latency. Whether these opportunities become
useful depends on where the zeros occur, their execution structure, and
the kernel used to exploit them. We evaluate the same specialized K050
implementation across all 30 Pythia-14M checkpoints to ask which forms of
learned sparsity translate into measured acceleration. Measurements use
BF16, batch-one, uncached 2,048-token inference with full-vocabulary logits
on one RTX5090. Each candidate undergoes numerical qualification against
the native reference on complete validation before timing
(Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results}).

\textbf{Model-wide sparsity is strongly associated with realized full-model
acceleration (Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-autoresearch}a).} Native-relative K050
speedup increases with $\Smodel$ across the cohort ($R^2=0.817$), reaching
$1.78\times$ for seven-site + OL1 at $\kappa=0.5$. The association remains
substantial within the baseline/one-site, four-site, and seven-site groups
(Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-diagnostics}). Thus, the representations produced
by the train-time interventions carry runtime-relevant sparsity; this does
not imply that each additional zero product produces a fixed latency reduction.

The aggregate relationship hides a distinction between where the additional
sparsity occurs. Higher thresholds produce substantial gains from projection
skipping, and OL1 further increases this benefit for the four-site recipe
at positive thresholds (Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-autoresearch}b). Without
pressure, four-site and seven-site recipes have similar projection-skipping
gains at matched thresholds. At $\kappa=0.5$, adding OL1 raises the four-site
gain from $1.324\times$ to $1.378\times$, but the seven-site gain only from
$1.316\times$ to $1.325\times$, despite its much larger increase in
$\Smodel$. Much of that additional seven-site sparsity lies in $QK/PV$
attention products (Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping}), outside the
projection path. Additional model-wide sparsity therefore need not bring
a proportional projection-skipping benefit.

Scalar sparsity also does not fully determine the projection benefit. The
kernel exploits zero structure at the level of matrix-multiply fragments,
with very sparse rows sometimes handled by scalar operations instead.
We call the resulting omitted matrix multiply-accumulate instructions
\emph{MMA bypasses}; a bypass need not eliminate all corresponding arithmetic.
Across the cohort, projection MMA bypass is more strongly associated with
projection-skipping gain ($R^2=0.946$) than scalar projection sparsity alone
($R^2=0.413$). The useful execution unit is coarser than an individual zero
activation. Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-diagnostics} gives the predicates,
counters, and matched latencies.

The attention path provides the complementary result. Seven-site
interventions expose many zero-operand $QK$ and $PV$ products, but the tested
attention-skipping path remains slower than its dense counterpart across
the cohort, even at the checkpoint with substantial additional MMA skipping.
\textbf{For these 14M measurements, high-threshold projection skipping is
profitable, whereas attention skipping is not.} This is a property of the
measured kernel, shapes, and hardware, rather than evidence that attention
sparsity is intrinsically unprofitable.

Native-relative speedup is not a pure measure of sparse-path savings: it
divides native latency by optimized K050 latency, and the native cost itself
differs across intervention families. Seven-site recipes can report larger
native-relative speedups even when their absolute K050 latency exceeds that
of the corresponding four-site model. Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-diagnostics}
therefore reports native, optimized, and matched-ablation latencies separately.

Developing an implementation to test these representation-level effects
required architecture-specific kernel work. We used a human-guided coding-agent
loop configured with GPT-6 Astra~\citep{openaigpt6astra} to propose, execute,
profile, and refine implementations, selecting K050 after numerical
qualification. Execution feedback supports this development
approach~\citep{ouyang2025kernelbench,luo2026agentic}. The search history,
upstream-kernel compatibility tests, and implementation ablations are in
Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results}. The agent serves as an implementation
tool; this study does not compare agent-assisted with human-only optimization.

Together, these results separate sparsity opportunity from sparsity
realization. $\Smodel$ captures computation logically exposed to zeros and
tracks end-to-end performance across the trained models, but the runtime
value of those zeros depends on their location and execution structure.
K050 profitably exploits high-threshold projection sparsity, with pressure
amplifying the four-site benefit, while the additional attention sparsity
remains overhead-limited in the tested implementation.
