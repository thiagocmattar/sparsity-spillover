\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Pressure, thresholds and site placement need to be evaluated together.
Site placement also changes the optimization regime: at the common
$\lambda=1$ in the 14M runs, seven-site pressure is almost always norm-limited,
whereas four-site pressure is almost never capped.
In the matched 14M comparisons, adding pressure ranges from improving both
loss and sparsity to exchanging quality for additional zeros. Extending the
intervention to internal attention can increase model-wide sparsity even
when FFN sparsity falls. The high-threshold seven-site recipe with pressure
improves both axes over the four-site recipe at all three sizes, but the
zero-threshold ordering changes. These results favor evaluating each
addition against its matched recipe, rather than choosing interventions
from activation sparsity alone.

The interpretation is supported by a connected pattern across experiments.
Matched comparisons identify how pressure's added effect changes with the
threshold; distribution measurements reveal which activations change; and
product counts explain their model-wide consequence. The high-threshold
A7-OL1 advantage recurs at all three sizes, while the zero-threshold reversals
identify where that relationship stops holding. Agreement across these
comparisons makes the result more informative than a favorable checkpoint
or an isolated sparsity percentage.

Product accounting and kernel timing answer different questions.
$\Smodel$ measures how much matrix-product work is logically exposed to zero
operands; the kernel study asks how much of that opportunity can be
profitably realized. The distinction matters across sites: high-threshold
projection sparsity produces structured work that K050 can skip with a net
latency benefit, while additional Q/K/V sparsity increases the logical
opportunity without producing profitable attention skipping at the tested
14M shapes. The computational value of a sparse representation therefore
depends not only on how many zeros it contains, but also on where they occur
and the granularity at which the implementation can exploit them.

Cross-size comparisons use a common token budget rather than equal tokens
per parameter, and 410M also uses a lower peak learning rate; the A0
optimization trajectories in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization}
show that the three sizes finish in different realized optimization regimes.
Accordingly, the scale experiment tests recurrence of within-size recipe
relationships rather than quality scaling with parameter count.
These conclusions concern MiniPile pretraining and complete recipes at the
larger sizes. The distribution diagnostics cover 14M and describe responses
rather than isolating a causal spillover mechanism. The kernel results use one RTX5090,
full-sequence inference, native SDPA and matched fusion controls. Transfer
to cached decoding, larger models and other hardware remains untested.
