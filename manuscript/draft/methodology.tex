\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Sparsification interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}

An intervention specifies thresholding sites $\mathcal{N}$, their nonlinearities
and thresholds, and pressure sites $\mathcal{P}$. The sets are independent
choices, although many evaluated recipes align them
(Figure~\ref{fig:architecture-sites}; Appendix~\ref{app:sites}).
We use ReLU and fixed elementwise maps
$G_{+,\kappa}(x)=x\mathbf{1}\{x\geq\kappa\}$ and
$G_{\pm,\kappa}(x)=x\mathbf{1}\{|x|\geq\kappa\}$, with $\kappa\geq0$.
The former removes negative values and positives below $\kappa$; the latter
preserves the signs and values of surviving magnitudes. At zero threshold,
their forward maps are ReLU and identity. Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}
gives boundary derivatives. Fixed thresholds require neither ranking nor
input-dependent moment estimates, but this does not establish a speed
advantage over selection-based methods.

Pressure uses the mean absolute activation in each targeted site--layer
tensor, after its nonlinearity, then averages these means equally.
Expanding the targets changes the mixture of site gradients. \emph{Naive L1}
optimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_1$, clipping the
combined gradient before AdamW.

\emph{Orthogonal L1} (OL1) keeps task optimization primary. After task-only
AdamW, it preconditions pressure using the task's second moments, removes
the component opposing the adaptive task direction under conflict, and
caps the auxiliary vector relative to that direction's norm. This adapts
gradient-conflict projection~\citep{yu2020pcgrad,hsieh2024bloop} while
extending the norm-limiting principle of Pythia's gradient clipping to an
auxiliary update. The two caps act in different spaces.

Once this relative cap binds, increasing the pressure weight $\lambda$
no longer increases the ideal correction. We use this conditional property
to motivate a fixed pressure mechanism, holding budget $b=1$ and multisite
$\lambda=1$ while varying threshold and placement. Saturation is recipe-dependent:
retained logs show frequent capping for A7-OL1 at 14M and 70M, but rarely
at 410M. Appendix~\ref{app:pressure} gives the protected-direction geometry,
its distinction from task-loss preservation, and the measured cap activity.

\subsection{Sparsity and architectural reach}
\label{sec:measurement}

\emph{Activation sparsity} is the exact-zero fraction at a named site.
\emph{Model-wide activation sparsity}, $\Smodel=N_0/N_{\mathrm{model}}$,
weights zeros by affected scalar multiplications. Each declared block product
gets one credit when either activation operand is zero; the denominator
also includes the dense LM head, which gets no zero credit. Integer counts
are pooled before division, using actual operands and causally valid pairs
in full, uncached sequences (Appendix~\ref{app:accounting}). We report
$100\Smodel\%$; this is multiplication opportunity, not runtime or total FLOPs.

\emph{Architectural reach},
$\Rarch=N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})/N_{\mathrm{model}}$, describes the
work structurally reached if the selected sites were entirely zero, counting
overlaps once. Architecture, sites and sequence length determine it, not
checkpoint quality. Natural zeros outside selected-site reach can contribute
to $\Smodel$, so $\Rarch$ need not bound every observation. Its gap to
observed sparsity is not sparsity available without quality loss
(Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling}).
