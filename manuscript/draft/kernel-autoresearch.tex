\subsection{When model-wide sparsity translates to speedup}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}
\label{sec:kernel-autoresearch}

\textbf{The kernel saves attention arithmetic but retains other costs.}
At 14M, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ with $\kappa=0.5$ bypasses 58\% of $QK$
and 67\% of $PV$ matrix instructions (Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-autoresearch}).
However, the kernel checks for zero tiles after loading their inputs, so
skipping those instructions does not avoid the input reads. It also still
computes softmax normalization and assembles the attention output: a zero
$QK$ score does not imply a zero attention probability. Detecting which
tiles can be skipped adds work of its own.

Matched timing tests show that the skipping overhead outweighs the arithmetic
savings. With projection skipping held fixed,
enabling $QK/PV$ skipping increases latency in all 35 tested 14M models.
For the endpoint above, full-model latency rises from 0.468 to 0.473\,ms.
The tests establish a net overhead, without separating its individual
sources. This is a limitation of the tested kernel; the 70M bypass counts
alone do not establish the same timing attribution
(Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-diagnostics}).
