\subsection{Quality--sparsity trade-offs}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

Local pressure and broader nonlinearities offer different quality--sparsity trade-offs
(Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}). With OL1 at $h$, the model can
improve validation loss while introducing modest model-wide sparsity:
at $\lambda=0.5$, A1-H-OL1 reaches $3.768\%$ sparsity at loss 5.1102,
compared with A0's loss of 5.2086. A0 post-hoc clipping reaches a similar
sparsity level, $3.832\%$ at target $p=0.3$, but with loss 5.3619. Thus,
similar aggregate sparsity can carry different quality costs depending on
the trained recipe and evaluation-time intervention.

Broader interventions reach much greater sparsity. A7-OL1 at $\kappa=0.5$
attains $27.483\%$ model-wide sparsity at loss 5.8294, accepting a quality
cost relative to A0. This comparison also changes computational reach:
the A0 clipping reference acts at $a,m,h,z$, whereas A7 additionally thresholds
the internal attention operands. At 14M, these site sets have analytic sparsity
ceilings of $12.833\%$ and $29.952\%$, respectively. This comparison changes
both the trained representation and the computation the selected sites can
affect. Matched pressure-on/off comparisons isolate pressure's contribution. Full endpoints
and clipping trajectories are given in Appendix~\ref{app:complete-results}.

In the local $h$-only sweep, OL1 is not uniformly better than naive L1:
their validation-loss differences are small, and the ordering reverses at
$\lambda=1$; the complete matched sweep is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:complete-results}, Table~\ref{tab:paired-effects-full}.

\subsection{Paired effect of adding pressure}
\label{sec:paired-results}

To isolate the marginal contribution of pressure from threshold strength,
we compare each OL1 recipe with its pressure-free counterpart at the
\emph{same} trained threshold $\kappa$
(Figure~\ref{fig:paired-intervention-effects}). Thus, the four-site contrasts
are A4-OL1 minus A4 and the seven-site contrasts are A7-OL1 minus A7;
changing $\kappa$ changes the operating point at which pressure is evaluated,
while retaining a pressure-free reference at each threshold.

\textbf{The added value of pressure changes sharply with both threshold and
target set.} For the four-site recipe, OL1 slightly improves validation loss
at $\kappa=0$ and $0.01$, while adding $0.598$ and $1.173$ percentage points
(pp) of model-wide sparsity. At larger thresholds, the sparsity increment
approaches $2.5$ pp but carries increasing quality cost, reaching $+2.498$ pp
for $+0.3783$ validation loss at $\kappa=0.5$. For the seven-site recipe, the
pressure effect remains modest through $\kappa=0.1$, where it adds $1.372$ pp
for only $+0.0008$ loss. At $\kappa=0.5$, however, the increment rises to
$+12.096$ pp for $+0.1265$ loss.

\textbf{At the highest threshold, seven-site pressure therefore produces
nearly five times the additional model-wide sparsity of four-site pressure,
while incurring roughly one third of its validation-loss increase.}

This difference characterizes the complete pressure target sets, not an
isolated effect of adding $q,k,v$. Seven-site OL1 introduces additional
pressure targets, and because the pressure objective equally averages
targeted site--layer tensors, expanding the target set also changes the
normalization and relative weighting of the existing targets. The larger
seven-site effect therefore cannot be attributed to attention placement alone.

\begin{samepage}
\textbf{The same pressure weight produces very different optimization regimes
across target sets} (Figure~\ref{fig:ol1-geometry}). We log global dot products
and norms of the AdamW-preconditioned task direction $u$ and pressure direction
$w$ at every optimizer step of the ten Pythia-14M multisite OL1 runs.
Pre-projection alignment is negative on $99.9\%$ of the resulting 7,120 step
records. We quantify the task-relative opposing component as
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{opp}}=
\frac{\max(0,-\langle u,w\rangle)}{\|u\|_2^2+\epsilon},
\]
which expresses the removed opposing component relative to the task-direction
norm. Pooled across thresholds, its median is $0.0095$ for four-site pressure
and $0.588$ for seven-site pressure. Consistently, at $\lambda=b=1$, the norm
cap is active on only $0.5\%$ of four-site steps but $97.0\%$ of seven-site
steps. Thus, seven-site OL1 operates almost entirely in a budget-limited
regime, whereas four-site OL1 is almost entirely uncapped. Because expanding
to seven sites also changes the pressure objective and its equal-tensor
normalization, these diagnostics characterize the complete target sets rather
than isolating the contribution of $q,k,v$ alone.
\end{samepage}

Pressure therefore cannot be assigned a single context-independent
sparsity--quality effect: its marginal contribution depends on the thresholded
representation and pressure target set. We next examine how the resulting
four- and seven-site recipes differ in their activation structure.

\subsection{Reshaping activation distributions}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}

More FFN zeros need not mean more model-wide sparsity
(Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping}). At
$\kappa=0.5$, A4-OL1 places $99.31\%$ of pooled FFN elements exactly at
zero, compared with $93.64\%$ for A7-OL1. Their attention distributions
differ more sharply: A4-OL1 has a narrow nonzero peak and only $0.22\%$
exact zeros, whereas A7-OL1 has $95.60\%$ exact zeros and nonzero density
outside the symmetric threshold gap. The omitted zero masses are reported
in Table~\ref{tab:density-mass}; a narrow density peak is not itself a large
exact-zero probability.

A4 does not directly threshold or pressure $q,k,v$, yet their distributions
change substantially. This is a nonlocal response under the complete A4-OL1
intervention, consistent with spillover. The comparison with A0 does not
isolate pressure's contribution from the other training changes. A7 directly
intervenes at these attention sites, so its reshaping there has a different
interpretation.

The operation counts show why this distinction matters model-wide. At the
same threshold, $QK$ and $PV$ contribute 16.77 sparsity points under A7-OL1,
versus 0.06 under A4-OL1 (Appendix~\ref{app:operation-results}). Several
projection contributions are smaller under A7-OL1, but its attention
contribution more than offsets them. Greater model-wide sparsity can
therefore coexist with less pooled FFN sparsity. The scale comparison below
tests whether the A7-OL1 advantage over A4-OL1 persists in larger models.

\subsection{Transfer across model sizes}
\label{sec:scale-results}

The high-threshold A7-OL1 advantage persists at all three model sizes
(Figure~\ref{fig:scale-transfer}). At $\kappa=0.5$, A7-OL1 has both lower
loss and greater model-wide sparsity than A4-OL1 at 14M, 70M and 410M
(Table~\ref{tab:cross-scale-recipe-contrasts}). The zero-threshold ordering
does not transfer unchanged: A4-OL1 is better on both axes at 14M and 70M,
whereas A7-OL1 is better at 410M. Persistence concerns a specific recipe
relationship, not the entire threshold response.

Raw sparsity must also be read against the changing workload. The A7 sparsity
ceiling rises from $29.95\%$ to $49.42\%$ and $87.25\%$ as the dense LM
head accounts for a smaller share of counted products. The bottom row uses
the same A7 denominator for every curve within a size:
$U_{\mathrm{arch}}=\Smodel/\Smax(\mathrm{A7})$. Because A7 reaches all
counted block operations, this ratio equals the block zero-product fraction
$\Sblock$. It removes the dense-head share while retaining the different
weights of operations within the blocks.

This is transfer of a complete-recipe comparison. The larger cohorts lack
the no-pressure A4/A7 controls needed to isolate pressure's contribution.
They share a token budget rather than tokens per parameter, and 410M uses
a lower peak learning rate. Its A0 loss is also higher than 70M's under this
budget; the plot should not be read as a quality scaling law.
