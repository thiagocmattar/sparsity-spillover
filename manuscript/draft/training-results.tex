\subsection{Quality--sparsity trade-offs}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

Local pressure and broader nonlinearities occupy different regimes
(Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}). The lowest-loss local endpoint,
A1-H-L1 at $\lambda=1$, reaches $3.949\%$ sparsity at loss 5.1023, versus
A0's 5.2086. A0 clipping at $p=0.3$ reaches similar sparsity, $3.832\%$,
but loss 5.3619. A7-OL1 at $\kappa=0.5$ instead reaches $27.483\%$ at
loss 5.8294, or $+0.6208$ over A0. Its $29.952\%$ architectural reach also
exceeds the four-site clipping reference's $12.833\%$; the comparison
changes both the representation and affected computation.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/01-v3-14m-overview-compact.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Local pressure reaches lower loss; broader recipes trade quality for sparsity.}
All 30 trained 14M endpoints are filled; dashed colored paths have pressure,
solid paths do not. Open markers show uniform four-site A0 clipping.
Six of ten clipping evaluations fall inside the displayed loss range;
Appendix~\ref{app:clipping-results} retains the full range. Lines join evaluated
settings, not Pareto envelopes. A7 has broader reach than the clipping sites.}
\label{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}
\end{figure}

The learned representation also changes the response to later clipping.
At 14M and matched target $p=0.6$, ReLU-pretrained A1-H plus clipping reaches
$7.893\%$ sparsity at loss 6.5215, versus A0 clipping's $7.764\%$ at 6.8212
(a 0.129 pp sparsity mismatch). Relative to each measured $p=0$,
clipping adds 1.2518 versus 1.6126 loss. This benefit overcomes ReLU's
initial $+0.0611$ loss cost here; it does not do so at every target.
Figure~\ref{fig:scale-transfer} shows the cross-size comparison.


\subsection{Paired intervention effects}
\label{sec:paired-results}

Pressure's additional value depends on threshold and targets
(Figure~\ref{fig:paired-intervention-effects}). Replacing GELU with ReLU
adds 2.714 sparsity percentage points (pp) for $+0.0611$ loss. Local pressure
lowers observed loss versus ReLU; changing naive L1 to OL1 lowers loss at
three of four matched weights but reverses at $\lambda=1$. This compares
complete update rules, including clipping and optimizer state, rather than
isolating conflict projection.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/02-v3-paired-effects-compact.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Pressure's marginal effect changes across recipes and thresholds.}
All 29 paired 14M deltas are treatment minus the named reference: loss above,
sparsity pp below. Labels sweep $\lambda$ or $\kappa$ with ordinal spacing;
the dash denotes the unswept ReLU replacement. Pressure additions fix
nonlinearities and threshold; other contrasts can change multiple factors
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Shared controls and one training seed do not
provide 29 independent replicates. All numerical deltas are in Table~\ref{tab:paired-effects-full}.}
\label{fig:paired-intervention-effects}
\end{figure}

For A4, adding OL1 improves both axes at $\kappa=0,0.01$, then adds
sparsity at increasing quality cost: 2.498 pp for $+0.3783$ loss at
$\kappa=0.5$. The corresponding A7 addition yields 12.096 pp for $+0.1265$;
at $\kappa=0.1$ it yields 1.372 pp for $+0.0008$, which does not establish
loss equivalence. Without pressure, A7 minus A4 at $\kappa=0.5$ adds
5.171 pp for $+0.0432$ loss. The larger pressured gain under A7 changes
the target-gradient mixture and, outside saturation, its scale; it cannot
be assigned solely to broader thresholding. A separate historical comparison
of A4 with pressure at $h$ only (Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-targets}) further
motivates separating pressure placement from threshold placement.


\subsection{Reshaping activation distributions}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}

More FFN zeros need not mean more model-wide sparsity
(Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping}). At $\kappa=0.5$, A4-OL1 places
$99.31\%$ of pooled $h,m$ elements at zero, versus $93.64\%$ for A7-OL1;
their attention zero fractions are $0.22\%$ and $95.60\%$.
A narrow nonzero density peak is therefore not a large zero probability.
A4's untargeted attention distributions change, but comparison with A0
does not isolate pressure's contribution; A7 intervenes there directly.
Appendix~\ref{app:density-results} retains all thresholds and tails.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/05-v5-activation-density-high-threshold.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A7-OL1 has fewer FFN zeros but far more attention zeros at $\kappa=0.5$.}
Exact-zero masses appear above nonzero densities. Counts pool all layers and
338 validation blocks, with $h:m$ weighting 80:20 and equal $q:k:v$ weighting,
and Q/K measured after RoPE. Density divides by all elements and bin width; the
symlog axis is linear below 0.01, so areas are not probabilities. Dotted lines
mark thresholds. The complete grid, precision and tails are in
Appendix~\ref{app:density-results}.}
\label{fig:activation-reshaping}
\end{figure}

Operation counts explain the ranking reversal (Table~\ref{tab:operation-summary}).
A7-OL1's $QK+PV$ contribution rises by 16.72 pp while its projection
contribution falls by 1.95 pp, producing greater model-wide sparsity despite
fewer FFN zeros. Reach identifies which work can be affected;
the measured counts establish how much has zero operands. Local FFN
sparsity alone therefore reverses the complete recipes' apparent ranking.

In separate BF16 diagnostics, entire $z$ rows are zero on $97.15\%$ of
rows for A4-OL1 and $91.20\%$ for A7-OL1. On those rows the output projection
returns its bias, if present. This does not zero the residual stream;
whether the remaining branch contributions retain meaningful context
dependence is an open question (Appendix~\ref{app:projection-inputs}).

\subsection{Transfer across model sizes}
\label{sec:scale-results}

The high-threshold recipe relationship recurs beyond 14M
(Figure~\ref{fig:scale-transfer}). At $\kappa=0.5$, A7-OL1 improves over
A4-OL1 by 0.2086, 0.1736 and 0.0703 loss and by 14.769, 5.006 and
9.024 sparsity pp at 14M, 70M and 410M. At zero threshold, A4-OL1 is
better on both axes at 14M/70M, while A7-OL1 is better at 410M.
Larger cohorts test complete recipes; they lack no-pressure A4/A7 controls.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/03-v4-cross-size-interventions.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{High-threshold A7-OL1 improves both axes over A4-OL1 at every size.}
Columns compare 14M, 70M and 410M; rows show absolute loss versus
$\Smodel$ and loss minus same-size A0 versus $\Sblock$.
Filled colored paths connect five separately trained thresholds; rings mark
$\kappa=0.5$. Open paths follow ten post-hoc targets on fixed GELU A0 or
ReLU A1-H checkpoints; filled gray markers show their unmodified endpoints.
There are 96 distinct evaluations, repeated across rows. Dashed guides show
A4/clipping and A7 reach. Top panels omit 6/3/5 high-loss clipping points;
bottom panels omit 6/5/6. Appendix~\ref{app:scale-curves} retains all ranges.
The token budget is common, with a lower learning rate at 410M.}
\label{fig:scale-transfer}
\end{figure}

A7-OL1 at $\kappa=0.5$ reaches $27.483\%$, $40.602\%$ and $80.616\%$
model-wide sparsity against A7 reaches of $29.952\%$, $49.424\%$ and
$87.245\%$. Part of this increase is architectural: the dense head's
share falls, and the block-operation mix changes. The normalized row divides
every curve by its size's common A7 reach, giving $\Sblock$; this removes
head share while retaining differences in block-operation weights.

ReLU pretraining followed by clipping also has a recurring favorable
region: at $p=0.5$, A1-H has lower loss and higher sparsity than A0 clipping
at every size. ReLU's initial loss costs are $+0.0611$, $+0.1230$ and
$+0.1038$, yet its incremental clipping damage is smaller at all positive
tested targets. Total loss still favors A0 at $p\leq0.3$ and returns to
favor A0 at $p=0.9$ for 14M and $p\geq0.8$ for 410M. ReLU's starting
zeros and the added four-site reach are distinct from sensitivity to
removing further values; a matched quantile is not matched achieved sparsity.

Absolute quality and dense-relative penalty follow different patterns.
A0 loss falls from 5.2086 to 4.0998, then rises to 4.5475 at 410M;
high-threshold A7-OL1 instead improves from 5.8294 to 5.2159 to 5.1207.
Its penalties over A0 are consequently $+0.6208$, $+1.1162$ and $+0.5732$.
The 14M/70M peak learning rates agree; 410M uses a lower rate and far fewer
tokens per parameter. Retained 410M A0 loss continues improving in the final
20\% of training, with median pre-clipping task norm 0.850 versus
0.317/0.295 at 14M/70M (Appendix~\ref{app:training-dynamics}). This supports
investigating a finite-budget explanation, without establishing its cause.
