\subsection{Exact activation locations and counting}
\label{app:counting}
Pythia uses parallel attention and feed-forward residual branches. Their
normalization outputs $a$ and $m$ are distinct tensors.
Table~\ref{tab:sites} specifies the operands used by the interventions and
counters. The attention output after $W_o$ is a separate diagnostic tensor.

\centering
\caption{Activation locations. $B$ is batch size, $H$ the number of heads,
$d_h=d/H$, and $d_f$ the feed-forward width. Pressure captures the selected
site's output after its gate.}
\label{tab:sites}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Site & Tensor location & Shape & Consuming product \\
\midrule
$a$ & attention-branch norm/gate output & $[B,T,d]$ & fused QKV \\
$m$ & feed-forward-branch norm/gate output & $[B,T,d]$ & $W_1$ \\
$h$ & feed-forward activation/gate output & $[B,T,d_f]$ & $W_2$ \\
$q,k$ & immediately after partial RoPE/gate & $[B,H,T,d_h]$ & $QK^\top$ \\
$v$ & values from fused QKV/gate & $[B,H,T,d_h]$ & $PV$ \\
$z$ & concatenated context/gate before $W_o$ & $[B,T,d]$ & $W_o$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For one sequence and $L$ layers, the denominator is
\begin{equation}
 N_{\rm model}
 =L\left[T(4d^2+2dd_f)+dT(T+1)\right]+TdV ,
 \label{eq:denominator}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is vocabulary size. The six block counts per layer are
$3Td^2$ (QKV), $Td^2$ ($W_o$), $Tdd_f$ each ($W_1,W_2$), and
$dT(T+1)/2$ each ($QK^\top,PV$).
For $QK^\top$, a multiplication is zero when
$q_{i,r}=0$ or $k_{j,r}=0$ with $j\leq i$.
For $PV$, the corresponding event is $p_{i,j}=0$ or $v_{j,r}=0$.
Future-token pairs are excluded from both numerator and denominator.
Projection counters evaluate the actual activation and weight operands.
Exact zero means equality to zero in the evaluation dtype.
All counts are summed over the 338 sequences before division.

The denominator credits one scalar multiplication per operand pair.
Its full-sequence causal convention is held constant across conditions;
implementation arithmetic can differ, including fused dense attention.
The dense vocabulary head is included in full with zero credited opportunity.
Appendix~\ref{app:runtime} separately reports the subset of model-wide
opportunity covered by the evaluated sparse linear implementation.

\subsection{Optimization}
\label{app:optimization}
The joint-loss L1 baseline differentiates
$\mathcal{L}_{\rm CE}+\lambda\mathcal{P}$, clips the combined gradient to
global norm one, and performs an AdamW update. OL1 accumulates task and
pressure gradients separately across the effective batch. It clips the task
gradient to norm one and updates AdamW's moments using that gradient.
With bias-corrected task moments $\widehat m,\widehat v$, it forms
\begin{equation}
 d_t=\frac{\widehat m}{\sqrt{\widehat v}+\epsilon_{\rm Adam}},
 \qquad
 d_p=\frac{\nabla_\theta\mathcal{P}}
 {\sqrt{\widehat v}+\epsilon_{\rm Adam}} .
\end{equation}
The inner product and norms below are global over trainable parameters.
With $\epsilon=10^{-12}$, when $\langle d_t,d_p\rangle<0$ and
$\|d_t\|_2^2>\epsilon$, the pressure direction is replaced by
\begin{equation}
 d_s=d_p-\frac{\langle d_t,d_p\rangle}
 {\|d_t\|_2^2+\epsilon}d_t;
\end{equation}
otherwise $d_s=d_p$. With step budget $b=1$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\frac{\lambda\|d_s\|_2}{\|d_t\|_2+\epsilon},
 \qquad c=\min\left(1,\frac{b}{\rho+\epsilon}\right).
\end{equation}
After AdamW's task update, each parameter group receives
$-\eta\lambda c\,d_s$, using its current learning rate $\eta$.
Weight decay remains part of AdamW. This procedure bounds the auxiliary
direction relative to the task direction at the current step. Its geometric
constraint leaves finite-step task-loss improvement as an empirical question.

\subsection{Clipping calibration and selection}
For each control, the calibration pass uses ten complete training-cache
blocks. At every selected layer/site, the target $p$ specifies a quantile of
absolute activation magnitude. The resulting fixed thresholds are reused
throughout validation. A0 and A1-H have separate calibration and zero-target
evaluations. The target $p$ is shared across sites; achieved exact-zero
fractions can differ because of the activation distributions and native
zeros. Each path is reported in full in Appendix~\ref{app:clipping}.
The common-loss table uses the best discrete point satisfying the stated
allowance relative to that size's zero-clipped A0 loss. The allowances are
descriptive summaries chosen for this analysis; a held-out downstream
evaluation would be needed to assess selected operating points independently.

\section{Fixed-budget 410M extension}
\label{app:410m}
The 410M cohort receives the same token exposure and number of updates as
the smaller models, with peak learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$.
It extends the two pressured recipes and both controls
(Table~\ref{tab:all410}). Its trained families differ from the smaller
models: increasing the gate threshold can improve loss while increasing
opportunity (Figures~\ref{fig:410} and~\ref{fig:doses}).
At $\kappa=.5$, A7+OL1@7 has loss 5.1207 and opportunity 80.62\%;
the A0 control has substantially lower loss, 4.5475.
Thus a within-family improvement leaves a considerable gap to the
unmodified model.

The exposure is 3.7 tokens per parameter, compared with 21.2 at 70M and
106.1 at 14M. This difference motivates a larger-data control before
interpreting the result as a model-size effect.
Compute-optimal allocations provide a rationale for scaling the data budget
\citep{hoffmann2022training}; they leave the cause of this cohort's
behavior unresolved.
The learning-rate screen in Table~\ref{tab:lr} supports the selected A0
learning rate within its tested grid. The remaining design differences
include architecture, exposure per parameter, and optimization trajectory.

\section{Correctness and execution study at 70M}
\label{app:runtime}
The execution study uses six checkpoints: A0, A1-H, and
A4+OL1@4/A7+OL1@7 at $\kappa=0,.5$.
The linear implementation is adapted from the sparse-kernel path released
with \citet{cetin2026sparser}. Exact signed packing preserves all nonzero
values; the original positive-input predicate would discard negative
activations in these models.
The hardware is an NVIDIA H100 NVL. Full-model comparisons use BF16,
sequence length 2,048, batch sizes 1 and 32, and uncached full-sequence
prefill with native dense scaled-dot-product attention.
They include packing and downstream linear execution, with compilation
and CUDA graphs disabled.

For A4/A7, the sparse path covers QKV, $W_1$, $W_2$, and $W_o$.
For A0/A1-H it covers only $W_2$. We report the covered opportunity as
$R_{\rm covered}=\sum_{o\in{\rm replaced}} Z_o/N_{\rm model}$,
using the same complete denominator as \Rmodel.
In particular, the A7 $\kappa=.5$ checkpoint has 40.60\% total opportunity
but 30.18\% covered by these linear replacements.

Seven paired timing blocks provide each reported full-model ratio.
The best case, A4+OL1@4 at $\kappa=.5$ and batch size 1, has median
$0.983496\times$ and 10th--90th percentile range
$[0.982588,0.985447]\times$ across timing blocks.
This range describes timing dispersion in the measured process.
An official SparseLM-0.5B positive control reaches $1.2876\times$,
showing that the environment can reproduce a benefit for the source
model/path.

Verification covers all seven tested linear shapes and every sentinel
checkpoint. Source-checkpoint FP16 validation losses reproduce exactly.
The largest full-validation BF16 dense--sparse loss discrepancy is
0.0006854, below the declared 0.001 tolerance.
BF16 runtime validation and the canonical counting pass have separate
precision provenance; the manuscript's quality frontiers use the canonical
same-pass counting losses.

The study also tests 108 representative linear cases. Both pack-plus-kernel
and kernel-only timing remain below parity; the best kernel-only ratio is
$0.8359\times$. Separate Q-only or V-only sparse-attention compositions
preserve the selected computation but use unfused per-head execution.
Their best tested ratio is $0.3612\times$.
These checks help locate the limitation beyond full-model packing alone,
while leaving open alternative kernels, fused attention, decoding workloads,
batching policies, hardware, and explicitly structured training.
The result is specific to the implemented path and measured workload.
