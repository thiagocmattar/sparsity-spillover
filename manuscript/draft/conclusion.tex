\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An intervention should be evaluated against the recipe to which it is added.
In the matched 14M comparisons, pressure can improve both observed loss and
sparsity at one threshold, then exchange quality for additional zeros at
another. Broader intervention at internal attention can also increase
model-wide zero-product counts while reducing pooled FFN sparsity. Assessing
an addition therefore requires its paired quality change and the operations
affected, alongside the number of zero activations.

Recipe comparisons, model selection under a loss budget, and kernel selection
require different references. The high-threshold seven-site pressured recipe
improves on its four-site counterpart at all three sizes, yet neither recipe
supplies the best point under the smallest tested A0-relative loss budgets.
Similarly, a kernel can accelerate a sparse checkpoint relative to native
execution while some of its skipping paths make it slower. A favorable paired
recipe result or a high sparsity percentage alone does not settle the other
selection decisions.

Architectural reach identifies which products the selected sites can affect;
actual-operand counts measure zeros in the learned model; kernel instrumentation
measures skipped instructions; and timing determines whether skipping pays.
The attention ablation separates these quantities concretely: many eligible
instructions are skipped, but dense attention is faster on every checkpoint.
Reporting native, matched-fusion and sparse-path controls is necessary to
identify where the observed acceleration comes from.

These conclusions concern one-seed MiniPile pretraining at a fixed token
budget, with a lower learning rate at 410M and selected complete recipes at
the larger sizes. Uniform four-site clipping is a limited post-hoc comparator.
Distribution diagnostics cover 14M; the kernel study uses one RTX5090 and
full-sequence inference, with canonical FP16 counts and BF16 timing. Two
high-value questions remain: whether the placement relationships replicate
when existing pressure coefficients are held fixed, and whether the same
execution trade-off persists at larger widths.
