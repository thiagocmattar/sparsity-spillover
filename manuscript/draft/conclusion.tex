\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Pressure's marginal effect depends on threshold and target mixture: it can
improve both quality and sparsity or exchange quality for further zeros.
ReLU pretraining likewise changes the response to later clipping, with a
favorable intermediate region that need not extend to extreme targets.
These interactions make paired quality changes more informative than a
recipe's maximum sparsity alone.

Architectural reach helps choose placement before training by identifying
the affected work. Observed counts then explain why fewer FFN zeros can
coexist with greater model-wide sparsity. The high-threshold A7-OL1 advantage
over A4-OL1 recurs beyond 14M, while lower-threshold orderings change.
Width, depth and sequence length affect the accounting differently, so
larger raw sparsity is not itself evidence of better adaptation.

Execution adds another decision: whole-fragment skips can lose despite
substantial scalar zero opportunity. The evidence covers one-seed pretraining,
selected larger-size recipes, uniform clipping and one 14M runtime workload.
The retained trajectories motivate testing training-budget sensitivity;
the cap audit motivates varying relative pressure targets rather than merely
rescaling a saturated vector. Both would sharpen which intervention to train,
while larger-width kernels would test when its counted zeros become profitable.
