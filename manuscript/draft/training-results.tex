\subsection{Quality--sparsity operating regimes}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

% Overview and numerical claims: analyses/024-2026-09-17-h-only-kernel-latency/observations/028-all-model-quality-sparsity.md;
% generating analysis: analyses/024-2026-09-17-h-only-kernel-latency/20_plot_quality_all_sizes.py.
\textbf{The interventions extend the attainable sparsity range, sometimes
close to the architectural ceiling, at a quality cost.}
Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-all-sizes} summarizes all 74 trained
conditions across the three model sizes. At $\kappa=0.5$,
$T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $27.48\%$, $40.60\%$, and $80.62\%$
model-wide sparsity at 14M, 70M, and 410M: $91.8\%$, $82.2\%$, and $92.4\%$
of their respective seven-site ceilings. Validation loss increases by
$0.62$, $1.12$, and $0.57$ relative to the same-size base model.

All 40/22/12 trained conditions at 14M/70M/410M, with a shared loss scale.
Colors distinguish executed recipes; lines connect separate threshold

Larger models do not uniformly have lower loss under this training budget.
The 410M base model finishes at loss 4.547, above 70M's 4.100.
Every model receives the same 712 optimizer steps and 1.493B input tokens,
but this gives 106.14, 21.20, and 3.68 tokens per parameter at 14M, 70M,
and 410M. The 410M learning-rate schedule also has a lower peak
($3\times10^{-4}$ rather than $10^{-3}$).
These differences may contribute to the non-monotonic base-model loss. We show the training loss and gradient norms during training at the Appendix~\ref{app:training-protocol}, Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization}.

Given this non-monotonic base-model quality and the differing optimization
regimes, we retain a limited recipe sweep at 410M and focus the detailed
pressure-placement and execution analysis on 14M and 70M.

\subsection{Paired effects of pressure placement}
\label{sec:paired-results}

% Source: analyses/024-2026-09-17-h-only-kernel-latency/observations/015-pressure-scope-caption.md;
% generating analysis: paper_effect_figures.py / 13_rebuild_paper_figures.py;
% adoption and checked claims: manuscript/draft/reviews/2026-09-18-paired-pressure/README.md.
\textbf{H-only pressure gives a more favorable quality--sparsity trade-off
at moderate thresholds in both model sizes.}
For $\kappa\leq0.1$, $P_h$ lowers loss relative to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ in
all 16 matched four-/seven-site comparisons at 14M and 70M
(Figure~\ref{fig:paired-intervention-effects}). The loss reduction is
$0.19$--$0.32$, with sparsity differing by at most $3.36$ percentage points.
At $\kappa=0.5$, all-site pressure instead gives substantially more sparsity,
especially for $T_7$; at 70M it also lowers the $T_7$ loss by $0.121$.

H-only pressure has lower measured latency in 18 of the 20 pairs, including
all ten 70M pairs. The two small reversals occur at 14M and $\kappa=0.5$:
all-site pressure is faster by $2.78\,\mu$s for $T_4$ and $0.52\,\mu$s for
$T_7$. Thus, the extra sparsity from broader pressure does not consistently
translate into lower latency. Detailed 14M pressure-budget diagnostics are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}.

\subsection{Pressure placement reshapes local sparsity}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}

\textbf{The activation response depends on where pressure is applied}
(Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping}). We report $m$ and $h$ separately:
pooling them weights the wider $h$ tensors four times as heavily and can hide
changes at the FFN input. At $\kappa=0.5$, four-site pressure expansion from
$P_h$ to $P_4$ raises the $m$ zero fraction from $66.44\%$ to $96.77\%$, while
$h$ remains about $99.94\%$ zero. Under seven-site thresholding, expanding
$P_h$ to $P_7$ changes $m$ from $67.41\%$ to $68.70\%$, but pooled Q/K/V zeros
from $29.52\%$ to $95.60\%$.

The operation decomposition explains why the last comparison produces much
more model-wide sparsity. Broad seven-site pressure exposes zeros in $QK/PV$,
which together contribute $16.77$ pp, compared with $0.06$ pp under broad
four-site pressure. Thus, even with fewer zeros at $m$, $T_7/P_7$ reaches
$27.48\%$ model-wide sparsity versus $12.71\%$ for $T_4/P_4$.
Site fractions and the amount of computation consuming them jointly determine
$\Smodel$; the bars do not measure runtime savings.

At $\kappa=0.05$, h-only pressure raises $h$ sparsity from about $81\%$ to
$98\%$ under both threshold scopes while leaving $m$ near $52\%$.
Under seven-site thresholding, pooled Q/K/V zeros also change from $3.62\%$
to $4.20\%$, despite those sites receiving no direct pressure. We call
changes at nonpressured sites a \emph{nonlocal response}; the matched
$P_0\rightarrow P_h$ comparison holds threshold scope fixed but does not
identify the internal pathway. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:moderate-structure}
and Figure~\ref{fig:pressure-layer-zeros} give the moderate-threshold and
site-by-layer counts. Signed densities remain available for the original
subset only (Appendix~\ref{app:density-results}); near-zero concentration
should not be confused with exact zeros.

\subsection{Pressure placement across model sizes}
\label{sec:scale-results}

\textbf{At $\kappa=0.5$, seven-site + OL1(all) has both lower validation loss
and greater model-wide sparsity than four-site + OL1(all) at all three model
sizes (Figure~\ref{fig:scale-transfer}).} The same ordering does not hold
at $\kappa=0$: four-site + OL1(all) is better on both axes at 14M and 70M,
whereas seven-site + OL1(all) is better on both axes at 410M. Thus, the cross-size
result is recurrence of the \textbf{high-threshold complete-recipe
relationship}, not transfer of the entire threshold response. This broad-pressure
comparison retains its original coverage. Exact matched seven-site minus four-site OL1(all) contrasts at all
five thresholds are reported in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:scale-pairs-full}.

% Source: Analysis024 observations/025-matched-quality-latency.md;
% generating analysis: analyses/024-2026-09-17-h-only-kernel-latency/16_plot_matched_quality_latency.py.
The 70M h-only extension in Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}c
allows pressure scope to be compared at a fixed threshold scope.
At $\kappa=0.5$, expanding $T_7$ pressure from $P_h$ to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$
increases $\Smodel$ from $32.233\%$ to $40.602\%$ and lowers loss from
5.33724 to 5.21598. At 14M, the corresponding expansion increases sparsity
from $16.664\%$ to $27.483\%$ but raises loss from 5.73205 to 5.82939.
Thus, the high-threshold sparsity increase recurs at 70M, while its quality
cost does not. Multisite no-pressure controls remain unavailable at 70M;
410M has neither h-only nor multisite no-pressure controls. These comparisons
do not isolate pressure's effect relative to no pressure at the larger sizes.

Raw $\Smodel$ must be interpreted relative to the computation reachable by
each architecture. The seven-site sparsity ceiling increases from $29.95\%$
at 14M to $49.42\%$ at 70M and $87.25\%$ at 410M, largely because the dense
output projection accounts for a smaller fraction of the counted products
as model width and depth increase. At $\kappa=0.5$, seven-site + OL1(all) reaches
$27.48\%$, $40.60\%$, and $80.62\%$ $\Smodel$, corresponding to
\textbf{91.8\%, 82.2\%, and 92.4\% of the respective seven-site ceilings}.
The much larger raw sparsity at 410M therefore reflects in part a larger
architectural opportunity for the selected intervention sites, rather than
an isolated effect of model size on learned zero fractions.

The common-step budget and optimization limits in
Section~\ref{sec:quality-sparsity-results} apply here: these comparisons test
the recurrence of within-size recipe relationships, rather than a
language-model quality scaling law.
