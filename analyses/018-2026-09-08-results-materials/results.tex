\subsection{Quality--sparsity trade-offs at 14M}
% Source: observations/O001-overview.md, O002-blocked-effects.md.
The detailed study includes 30 trained conditions with matched initialization
and data-order seed 1234, 712 optimizer updates and 1,493,172,224 input tokens.
Training uses FP16 dynamic loss scaling, FP32 parameters and AdamW. Each
endpoint pairs loss and model-wide sparsity in the same complete eager
validation pass over 338 blocks from 500 documents, excluding a 1,444-token tail.
The full cohort spans distinct trade-off regimes: local L1 at
$\lambda=1$ gives the lowest observed loss, 5.1023 at 3.9493\% sparsity, while
A7-OL1 at $\kappa=.5$ reaches 27.4827\% at loss 5.8294.

Pressure's added effect depends on threshold and topology
(Figure~\ref{fig:a018-effects}). At fixed A4 gates, OL1 improves both outcomes
at $\kappa=0$ and $.01$, but at $.5$ adds 2.4979 sparsity points for a .3783
loss increase. At A7, OL1 instead adds 12.0959 points for a .1265 loss increase
at $.5$. At $.1$, its gain is 1.3717 points for a .000819 loss increase.
These are one-seed differences, not significance or equivalence claims.

Within a fixed topology, only its specified pressure objective is added.
Across topologies the objective's sites and normalization differ. The
no-pressure A4/A7 contrast at $\kappa=0$ has a small numerical residual
($-.002123$ loss, $+.005632$ sparsity points) despite identity Q/K/V gates;
it is not interpreted as a benefit of an identity transformation.

\subsection{Architecture reach and selected-recipe transfer}
% Source: observations/O008-ceiling-vs-model-size.md, O003-scale-transfer.md.
The analytic ceiling separates reachable workload from learned zero rate
(Figure~\ref{fig:a018-ceiling}). At uncached $T=2048$, A7's all-block ceiling
is 29.952\%, 49.424\% and 87.245\% at 14M, 70M and 410M. The dense output
head accounts for a much larger fraction of the counted workload at 14M.
OL1 changes training but not the selected-topology ceiling.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\resultsassets/figures/08-ceiling-vs-model-size.pdf}
\caption{Analytic all-zero reach ceilings at the three retained architectures.
The x axis uses actual parameter counts on a logarithmic scale, labeled by
nominal size. Counts assume one complete uncached $T=2048$ sequence and retain
the dense vocabulary head in the denominator. OL1 variants share their gate
topology's ceiling. A0 selects no intervention sites and has zero reach.
Lines connect computed architectures, not a fitted scaling law.}
\label{fig:a018-ceiling}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:a018-scales} retains absolute quality above and uses
$U_{\mathrm{arch}}=R_{\mathrm{model}}/R_{\mathrm{model}}^{\max}$ below.
The high-threshold ordering transfers: at $\kappa=.5$, A7-OL1 improves on
A4-OL1 by 14.7692, 5.0057 and 9.0241 sparsity points while reducing loss by
.2086, .1736 and .0703. At $\kappa=0$, A4-OL1 is better on both outcomes at
14M/70M, while A7-OL1 is better at 410M. The larger cohorts lack no-pressure
A4/A7 and therefore test complete recipes, not the isolated pressure effect.
The fixed token budget gives 106.1, 21.2 and 3.7 tokens/parameter; the 410M
peak learning rate is $3\times10^{-4}$ versus $10^{-3}$ at 14M/70M.

Ceiling utilization is explanatory, not a size-invariant quality or speed
score. A4-OL1 realizes more of its narrower ceiling at every matched dose
and size; this does not reverse the high-threshold raw advantage of A7-OL1.
Natural zeros outside selected reach remain in the numerator, so utilization
need not be bounded by one. Unmodified A0 has an undefined ratio. The
reach-restricted sensitivity measure is retained separately in the tables.

\subsection{Exact-zero mass and attention operands}
% Source: observations/O005-activation-case-study.md.
% Finer magnitude bins requested by the user still require a new diagnostic pass.
At $\kappa=.5$, both recipes exceed 99.8\% exact zeros at FFN hidden site $h$.
Their attention operands differ (Figure~\ref{fig:a018-activation}): A4-OL1
has 47.64\% of query values and 56.12\% of key values within magnitude .01,
but fewer than .001\% exact zeros at either site. A7-OL1 instead gives
93.54\% query zeros, 94.54\% key zeros and 98.71\% value zeros.
Query RMS is lower under A4-OL1 (.146 versus .483), illustrating that smaller
magnitudes alone do not identify exact-zero opportunity.

Marginal mass does not identify a causal spillover mechanism. The retained
artifacts contain counts and moments, not raw samples or signed densities.
Operation contributions and the complete post-hoc comparison supply supporting
context in Figures~\ref{fig:a018-operations} and~\ref{fig:a018-clipping}.

\subsection{Qualified kernel realization}
% Source: observations/O007-kernel-realization.md; revised 30-checkpoint cohort.
The qualified incumbent reaches 1.7830$\times$ on fixed c30 across 42 eligible
proposals (Figure~\ref{fig:a018-kernel}). Final K050 qualifies on all 30
included checkpoints, with a 1.2340$\times$ geometric mean speedup and a
1.0059--1.7832$\times$ range. The descriptive sparsity--speedup fit has
$R^2=.8167$. Sparse paths provide a 1.0432$\times$ geometric mean ratio over
the fused no-skip ablation, but help only 14/30 checkpoints. Disabling
attention skipping improves all 30, with 1.2506$\times$ mean speedup.
P0 qualifies on only four checkpoints, preventing a whole-cohort ranking.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\resultsassets/figures/07-kernel-realization.pdf}
\caption{Matched kernel evidence restricted to the 30 included checkpoints.
Left: qualified incumbent on fixed A7-OL1 $\kappa=.5$, initialized at native

OLS fit with an intercept ($R^2=.8167$); dotted lines mark native 1$\times$.
Timings use one RTX5090, BF16, batch one, uncached $T=2048$, full-vocabulary
logits, 64 fixed inputs and three fresh processes. Numerical qualification
covers all 338 validation blocks; the denominator is same-checkpoint native

\label{fig:a018-kernel}
\end{figure}

Canonical sparsity is measured in FP16 while execution uses BF16. Quality,
weights and gates differ across checkpoints; the correlation does not establish
causal proportionality or equal-quality acceleration. This workload does not
establish cached-decoding or larger-model speedup.
