# Sparse vocabulary-head latency

**Question:** Does the new consumer accelerate the vocabulary head and full forward of the four frozen Run066 models?

**Method and coverage:** One physical RTX5090, three fresh processes per model, 64 fixed inputs × 7 paired passes per process. B1/T2048; unchanged FP32 parameters, FP32 8x16 corner gate and FP16 projection. Every full graph qualifies against its own unmodified eager model over all500 documents/338 complete blocks,692224 inputs/691886 predictions;1444 tail tokens excluded. Separate head graphs qualify on every timed operand.

**Figure and caption:** [PDF](../figures/01-sparse-vocabulary-head.pdf). Three separately measured latency boundaries, with zero-based axes. The head retains the vermillion vocabulary-projection color used in Analysis042. Complete forward is gray. Fill/hatching identifies execution: solid dense cuBLAS, diagonal custom no-skip, crossed custom sparse. Black dots show each of the three process geometric means, with horizontal offsets for visibility. The two head panels share a y-axis scale; complete forward has its own scale. Every output vocabulary entry is computed.

**Result:** See [the complete table](../results/REPORT.md) and [paired/process data](../results/comparison.json). Positive latency reductions indicate lower latency; negative reductions indicate a slowdown. Report both the cuBLAS comparison and the same-custom-kernel skip control.

**Caveats:** Component boundaries are not additive decomposition segments. Projection-only excludes recurring activation preparation. The M16 consumer skips a K16 group only when both constituent M8 gate tiles are empty; actual bypass differs from 8x16 gate sparsity. Source-level avoided weight loads do not establish measured DRAM savings. Local development timings are excluded. No cached decoding, larger-model or multi-training-seed claim follows.

**Sources:** [20_analyze.py](../20_analyze.py) reduces verified returned artifacts; [23_figure.py](../23_figure.py) generates this PDF. Frozen checkpoint/runtime/cache identities are in provenance/inputs.json. No manuscript modification is included.

## Verified numerical interpretation

All 12 full processes and all head graphs match their native logits/loss exactly. The custom sparse head saves 11.43-16.79% versus custom no-skip, but is 35.37-44.55% slower than dense cuBLAS; full forward is 7.19-8.86% slower. All three processes reproduce the slowdown. Empty 8x16 gate tiles are 17.34-27.42%; the paired 16x16 consumer bypasses only 7.07-15.92%. The baseline custom execution penalty outweighs skipping savings. See [diagnostic tables and process ranges](../results/DIAGNOSTICS.md), generated by [22_diagnostic_report.py](../22_diagnostic_report.py). The two head panels share their y-axis scale; full forward has its own scale. The separately measured boundaries do not form an additive decomposition.
