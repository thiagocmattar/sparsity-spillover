\subsection{Results at 70M}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency} shows that the 70M model follows the same broad pattern observed at 14M: stronger interventions extend the attainable sparsity range, but at increasing quality cost. At $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $27.48\%$ and $40.60\%$ model-wide sparsity at 14M and 70M, corresponding to $91.8\%$ and $82.2\%$ of their respective ceilings, while validation loss increases by $0.62$ and $1.12$ relative to the base models. At moderate thresholds ($\kappa\leq0.1$), pressure confined to $h$ maintains lower loss than global pressure at similar sparsity.

\textbf{The 14M kernel does not transfer efficiently to 70M.}
The direct adaptation of the 14M kernel to 70M can exploit the additional sparsity to reduce inference latency, but its overhead largely offsets these gains. In the matched sweep, the original port takes 3.325\,ms on the base model, compared with 1.611\,ms for native PyTorch, and its best sparse recipe reaches only $1.013\times$ the native base-model speed. Additional optimization reduces repeated sparsity checks at $h,z$, uses a scalar path for very sparse rows, and improves dense projections, attention and the output head. On $T_7/P_h$ at $\kappa=0.5$, this reduces latency from 1.590 to 1.088\,ms ($1.481\times$ the native base-model speed). All 26 checkpoints pass full-validation numerical checks in three fresh processes (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-transfer}). The lower-loss $T_2/P_h$ points, however, remain slower than native: loss is only $0.029$--$0.093$ above Base, but optimized latency is 1.956--2.504\,ms. Thus, the favorable 14M quality--latency balance does not transfer directly.

\textbf{More sparsity does not necessarily imply lower latency.}
Within a fixed recipe, increasing sparsity generally reduces latency, but model-wide sparsity alone is not predictive. At 14M and $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ is substantially sparser than $T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ ($27.48\%$ versus $12.71\%$), yet slightly slower (0.473 versus 0.459\,ms). The 70M results show the same dependency on implementation: additional sparsity generally lowers latency within each recipe, but even the optimized implementation remains slower than native for many checkpoints. Speedup therefore depends on where zeros occur, whether they form groups the kernel can skip, and the cost of exploiting that structure.

\textbf{Post-hoc sparsity does not automatically translate into speedup.}
Post-hoc clipping reaches at most $1.025\times$ the native base-model speed at 14M (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results}); this small difference spans GPU sessions and does not establish a reliable clipping benefit. In our implementation, clipping adds a separate masking pass, and the resulting zeros must still form groups that the kernel can skip. Since we did not optimize fused clipping, these results characterize the current execution path rather than a general limit of post-hoc sparsification.

\subsection{Spillover: Local pressure induces nonlocal sparsity}
\label{sec:paired-results}

\textbf{Local pressure produces a nonlocal response.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pressure-layer-sparsity} present the sparsity by site and layer accross $T_4$ and $T_7$, for all pressures at 14M. 
At $\kappa=0.05$, applying pressure only at $h$ increases $h$ sparsity from 75--85\% to 94--99\% under $T_4$, and from 74--85\% to 94--99\% under $T_7$.
The response is not confined to the pressured site: sparsity at the unpressured $z$ activation rises from 71--85\% to 83--92\% under $T_4$, and from 70--87\% to 84--94\% under $T_7$, while changes at $a$ and $m$ are comparatively small.
Under $T_7$, broadening pressure to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ does not further increase sparsity at $h$ or $z$; instead, both become less sparse than under $P_h$ across all layers. One possible explanation is that (q,k,v) increases $\ell_1$ pressure norms, and $P_{all}$ caps the OL1 norm more frequently (Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}), reducing the local pressure at $h$.
Thus, a local site pressure may spillover and affect the distributions of other sites, and we speculate that it sparsify sites more succeptible to sparsification.
