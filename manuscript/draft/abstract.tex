\begin{abstract}
Activation sparsity can accelerate language-model inference when specialized kernels skip computation associated with zero activations. Existing methods obtain sparsity through different combinations of training-time pressure, thresholding, and placement, but their relative and combined effects have not been isolated under a common setup. We study these interventions under matched pretraining conditions across Pythia models. To measure their computational potential,
we define model-wide activation sparsity $\Smodel$ as the fraction of forward matrix-multiplication products with a zero activation operand, together with an architecture-dependent sparsity ceiling $\Smax$.
\textbf{Pressure placement changes the quality--sparsity trade-off}: local FFN-hidden pressure gives favorable moderate-threshold operating points in both the 14M and 70M sweeps, lowering loss in all 16 matched comparisons, while broad seven-site pressure becomes useful for extreme sparsity. The most aggressive recipes approach their respective sparsity ceilings at a cost in quality. Selected trained recipes also outperform uniform post-hoc thresholding at high sparsity across all model sizes.
For the implemented kernels on an RTX5090, \textbf{larger logical sparsity need not imply lower optimized latency}: most savings in the 14M ablation come from skipping FFN-down and attention-output projections. These results show that activation sparsification is governed by the interaction between how zeros are induced, where they are placed, and how much of that zero structure can be exploited by sparse execution.
\end{abstract}
