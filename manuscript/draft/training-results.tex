\subsection{Quality--sparsity operating regimes}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

\textbf{Narrow pressure is favorable at moderate thresholds, while broad
seven-site pressure exposes the highest sparsity} (Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}).
For both threshold scopes at $\kappa\leq0.1$, h-only OL1 lowers validation loss
relative to no pressure by $0.18$--$0.26$. At $\kappa=0.05$, $T_7/P_h$ reaches
$10.126\%$ model-wide sparsity at loss 5.19496, compared with A0's 5.2086.
This is comparable loss at the evaluated seed, not evidence of improved generalization.
Broad seven-site pressure is not uniformly preferable: at this threshold,
$T_7/P_7$ has both higher loss (5.46280) and lower sparsity ($9.863\%$).

At $\kappa=0.5$, the trade-off changes. Broad seven-site pressure reaches
$27.483\%$ model-wide sparsity, compared with $16.664\%$ under h-only pressure,
at losses 5.82941 and 5.73205. This high-sparsity operating point uses
$91.8\%$ of the seven-site ceiling, with a quality cost relative to A0.
It is distinct from the moderate-threshold regime in which narrow pressure is favorable.

In the local ReLU sweep, OL1 is not uniformly better than naive L1:
their validation-loss differences are small, and the ordering reverses at
$\lambda=1$ (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:paired-effects-full}). Full trained endpoints
appear in Table~\ref{tab:trained-endpoints}. Post-hoc thresholding at $a,m,h,z$
has four-site reach; it does not share the seven-site recipe's attention coverage.

\subsection{Paired effects of pressure placement}
\label{sec:paired-results}

We hold threshold scope and $\kappa$ fixed and separate two pressure increments:
$P_0\rightarrow P_h$, then $P_h\rightarrow P_{\mathrm{all}}$, where
$P_{\mathrm{all}}$ is $P_4$ or $P_7$ (Figure~\ref{fig:paired-intervention-effects}).
The first increment supplies the large moderate-threshold quality improvement.
Broadening pressure then raises loss at every tested threshold, but its
sparsity increment depends strongly on the operating point. For seven-site
thresholding it is negative through $\kappa=0.05$, then rises to $10.819$ pp
at $\kappa=0.5$ for $+0.0974$ loss. At the same high threshold, broadening
four-site pressure adds $2.486$ pp for $+0.3153$ loss.

\textbf{At $\kappa=0.5$, expanding seven-site pressure from $h$ to all sites
adds $10.82$ pp sparsity for $+0.0974$ validation loss.}
These contrasts separate pressure scope from threshold scope. They do not
isolate Q/K/V pressure: $T_7/P_4$ is absent, so $P_h\rightarrow P_7$ adds
$a,m,z,q,k,v$ jointly. It also changes the equal-tensor normalization of the
pressure objective. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:pressure-scope-full} gives every increment.

\textbf{Pressure scope also changes the realized optimization regime}
(Figure~\ref{fig:ol1-geometry}). All 20 pressured multisite runs retain 712
optimizer-step records. We measure the task-relative opposing component
$\rho_{\mathrm{opp}}=\max(0,-\langle u,w\rangle)/(\|u\|_2^2+\epsilon)$
in the global AdamW-preconditioned geometry. H-only pressure is capped on
$0.25\%$ of steps under either threshold scope; broad four-site pressure is
capped on $0.51\%$, versus $96.99\%$ for broad seven-site pressure.
The pooled median pre-cap ratio $r/b$ is 0.102 and 0.086 for four-/seven-site
h-only pressure, compared with 0.253 and 57.49 for broad pressure. These logs
establish different magnitude and budget regimes; they do not identify the
cause of the validation-loss differences.

\subsection{Broader threshold placement under matched pressure scope}
\label{sec:qkv-thresholding-results}

without pressure and then with pressure fixed at $h$
(Table~\ref{tab:qkv-thresholding}). \textbf{At $\kappa=0.5$, broader thresholding

\subsection{Pressure placement reshapes local sparsity}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}

\textbf{The activation response depends on where pressure is applied}
(Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping}). We report $m$ and $h$ separately:
pooling them weights the wider $h$ tensors four times as heavily and can hide
changes at the FFN input. At $\kappa=0.5$, four-site pressure expansion from
$P_h$ to $P_4$ raises the $m$ zero fraction from $66.44\%$ to $96.77\%$, while
$h$ remains about $99.94\%$ zero. Under seven-site thresholding, expanding
$P_h$ to $P_7$ changes $m$ from $67.41\%$ to $68.70\%$, but pooled Q/K/V zeros
from $29.52\%$ to $95.60\%$.

The operation decomposition explains why the last comparison produces much
more model-wide sparsity. Broad seven-site pressure exposes zeros in $QK/PV$,
which together contribute $16.77$ pp, compared with $0.06$ pp under broad
four-site pressure. Thus, even with fewer zeros at $m$, $T_7/P_7$ reaches
$27.48\%$ model-wide sparsity versus $12.71\%$ for $T_4/P_4$.
Site fractions and the amount of computation consuming them jointly determine
$\Smodel$; the bars do not measure runtime savings.

At $\kappa=0.05$, h-only pressure raises $h$ sparsity from about $81\%$ to
$98\%$ under both threshold scopes while leaving $m$ near $52\%$.
Under seven-site thresholding, pooled Q/K/V zeros also change from $3.62\%$
to $4.20\%$, despite those sites receiving no direct pressure. We call
changes at nonpressured sites a \emph{nonlocal response}; the matched
$P_0\rightarrow P_h$ comparison holds threshold scope fixed but does not
identify the internal pathway. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:moderate-structure}
and Figure~\ref{fig:pressure-layer-zeros} give the moderate-threshold and
site-by-layer counts. Signed densities remain available for the original
subset only (Appendix~\ref{app:density-results}); near-zero concentration
should not be confused with exact zeros.

\subsection{Cross-size recurrence of broad-pressure recipes}
\label{sec:scale-results}

\textbf{At $\kappa=0.5$, seven-site + OL1(all) has both lower validation loss
and greater model-wide sparsity than four-site + OL1(all) at all three model
sizes (Figure~\ref{fig:scale-transfer}).} The same ordering does not hold
at $\kappa=0$: four-site + OL1(all) is better on both axes at 14M and 70M,
whereas seven-site + OL1(all) is better on both axes at 410M. Thus, the cross-size
result is recurrence of the \textbf{high-threshold complete-recipe
relationship}, not transfer of the entire threshold response. The larger
cohorts lack the h-only and pressure-free controls needed to isolate pressure's
contribution or establish h-only transfer. Exact matched seven-site minus four-site OL1(all) contrasts at all
five thresholds are reported in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:scale-pairs-full}.

Raw $\Smodel$ must be interpreted relative to the computation reachable by
each architecture. The seven-site sparsity ceiling increases from $29.95\%$
at 14M to $49.42\%$ at 70M and $87.25\%$ at 410M, largely because the dense
output projection accounts for a smaller fraction of the counted products
as model width and depth increase. At $\kappa=0.5$, seven-site + OL1(all) reaches
$27.48\%$, $40.60\%$, and $80.62\%$ $\Smodel$, corresponding to
\textbf{91.8\%, 82.2\%, and 92.4\% of the respective seven-site ceilings}.
The much larger raw sparsity at 410M therefore reflects in part a larger
architectural opportunity for the selected intervention sites, rather than
an isolated effect of model size on learned zero fractions.

Absolute validation loss is not monotonic with model size under the realized
protocol: the A0 endpoints are 5.2086, 4.0998, and 4.5475 for 14M, 70M, and
410M. This experiment holds the input-token budget fixed rather than tokens
per parameter; the resulting budgets are 106.14, 21.20, and 3.68 tokens per
parameter, and 410M also uses a lower peak learning rate. Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization} reports the corresponding A0 optimization
trajectories. \textbf{We therefore use the size sweep to test whether
within-size recipe relationships recur, not as a language-model quality
scaling law.}
