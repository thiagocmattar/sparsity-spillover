\section{Intervention definitions}
\label{app:interventions}

\subsection{Thresholding nonlinearities}

For finite $\kappa\geq0$, both thresholding nonlinearities retain equality and leave retained values unchanged.
The comparison mask is detached: the derivative with respect to the input
is one for retained entries and zero for rejected entries. Consequently,
$G_{+,0}$ and ReLU have identical forward values but differ at zero, where
their derivatives are one and zero, respectively. $G_{\pm,0}$ is exactly the
identity in both values and gradients. These nonlinearities use neither a straight-through
estimator nor a top-$k$ selection rule. Unselected sites retain their original transformations.

\subsection{Activation pressure and OL1}
\label{app:pressure}

\textbf{Objective and update.}
For $J$ activation tensors $A_j$ at the sites and layers targeted by
$\mathcal P$, each with $n_j$ entries after any thresholding, define
\begin{equation}
\ell_1(\theta;\mathcal P)=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{n_j}
\sum_{i=1}^{n_j}|A_{j,i}|.
\end{equation}
Each tensor has equal weight, so changing $\mathcal P$ changes both the
targets and normalization. Ordinary L1 applies AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2019adamw}
to the globally clipped gradient of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}}+\lambda\ell_1$.
OL1 separates task and pressure updates: task-only AdamW is followed by a
pressure correction that removes the opposing component and limits its norm.
Pressure therefore does not directly enter Adam's moment estimates.

Both gradients are evaluated at the same parameters and averaged over the
same microbatches. The clipped task gradient updates AdamW's bias-corrected
moments $\widehat m_t,\widehat v_t$; the pressure gradient
$g_1=\nabla_\theta\ell_1$ is unclipped and has no momentum. Using the same
preconditioner for both directions gives
\begin{equation}
u=\frac{\widehat m_t}{\sqrt{\widehat v_t}+\epsilon_A},
\qquad
w=\frac{g_1}{\sqrt{\widehat v_t}+\epsilon_A}.
\end{equation}
Norms and inner products include parameters with both gradients and initialized
Adam state. For $c=\langle u,w\rangle$, $q=\|u\|_2^2$ and stabilizer $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde w=
\begin{cases}
w-\dfrac{c}{q+\epsilon}u, & c<0\ \text{and}\ q>\epsilon,\\[3pt]
w, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ol1-projection}
\end{equation}
Aligned pressure is retained. For weight $\lambda\geq0$ and relative norm budget $b>0$,
\begin{equation}
r=\frac{\lambda\|\widetilde w\|_2}{\|u\|_2+\epsilon},
\qquad
s=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{b}{r+\epsilon}\right\}\quad(r>0),
\end{equation}
with $s=1$ when $r=0$. After task-only AdamW, each parameter group $p$ receives
$\theta_p\leftarrow\theta_p-\alpha_p\lambda s\widetilde w_p$, where
$\alpha_p$ is its learning rate. Before learning-rate scaling and ignoring
stabilizers, the correction norm is
$\min\{\lambda\|\widetilde w\|_2,b\|u\|_2\}$. At a fixed optimizer state,
increasing $\lambda$ has no further effect once the cap is active.

OL1 combines conflict projection~\citep{yu2020pcgrad} and a smoothed task
direction~\citep{hsieh2024bloop} in Adam's preconditioned coordinates.
The stabilizer makes orthogonality approximate. This calculation excludes
group learning rates and decoupled weight decay; avoiding opposition to the
Adam direction does not guarantee unchanged task or validation loss.

\textbf{Update geometry and budget saturation.}
\label{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}
We measure $\rho_{\mathrm{opp}}=\max(0,-c)/(q+\epsilon)$, the removed
component's norm relative to $\|u\|_2$ before capping. All recorded steps
satisfy $q>\epsilon$; aligned steps contribute zero. Figure~\ref{fig:ol1-geometry}

Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}, using all 712 updates per condition

$T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ steps, compared with $0.51\%$ for
$T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ and $0.25\%$ for either $P_h$ recipe.
The diagnostics are consistent with stronger pressure--task conflict when
attention sites are included, but do not isolate each site's contribution.

\subsection{Activation and model-wide sparsity}

Activation sparsity is the fraction of zero entries at a site, pooling
counts across layers and validation sequences before dividing.

Model-wide sparsity counts scalar multiplications with a zero activation input:
\begin{equation}
\Smodel=\frac{N_0}{N_{\mathrm{model}}}.
\end{equation}
$N_0$ counts these multiplications within Pythia blocks;
$N_{\mathrm{model}}$ counts all multiplications in those blocks and the
final vocabulary projection. The latter contributes only to the denominator.
Counts are pooled across layers and validation sequences; percentages are
$100\Smodel$.

For a block's linear projection from $d_{\mathrm{in}}$ to
$d_{\mathrm{out}}$ features over $n$ rows, the denominator contribution is
$n d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}$. Each zero input contributes
$d_{\mathrm{out}}$ to $N_0$, one multiplication per output feature.
Zero weights do not count as activation sparsity.

In attention, both operands are activations. We count each multiplication
once if either or both are zero. For queries $q$, keys $k$, values $v$ and
attention probabilities $p$, one head in one sequence contributes
\begin{align}
N_{0,\mathrm{QK}}&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{u=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{d_h}
\mathbf{1}\{q_{tj}=0\ \lor\ k_{uj}=0\},\label{eq:qk-count}\\
N_{0,\mathrm{PV}}&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{u=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{d_h}
\mathbf{1}\{p_{tu}=0\ \lor\ v_{uj}=0\}.\label{eq:pv-count}
\end{align}
Here $T$ is sequence length, $d_h$ the head dimension, $t$ the query position
and $u$ the key/value position. Restricting $u\leq t$ excludes masked future
positions from both counts. We sum over heads, layers and validation sequences,
using the actual operands after thresholding and intervening transformations.

Normalization, softmax, activation functions, additions and memory transfers
are excluded, so $\Smodel$ does not directly measure latency or speedup.

\subsection{Model-wide sparsity ceiling}
\label{app:ceiling}

Assume all activations at the thresholding sites $\mathcal{N}$ are zero.
If $N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})$ counts the multiplications that must
then have a zero activation input, the ceiling is
\begin{equation}
\Smax=\frac{N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})}{N_{\mathrm{model}}}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is unchanged, and each multiplication is counted once.
The numerator includes downstream activations forced to zero: setting
$v=0$ also makes $z=0$, affecting both $PV$ and the attention-output projection.
Zero attention scores do not imply zero softmax probabilities.

The ceiling depends only on architecture, selected sites and sequence length.
Natural zeros outside those sites can raise measured $\Smodel$ above it;
the ceiling does not predict the associated quality cost.
Appendix~\ref{app:architecture-counts} gives the counts for each Pythia size.
