## Related work chain-of-argument

### 1. Sparsification interventions
(i) Chain-of-argument:
- TEAL -> do train-free post-hoc thresholding and show that sparsity may map into speedup gains. Limitation: do not show train-time interventions -> TEAL set up the motivation for apply train-time intervention and improve over the quality-computation trade-off; 
- Prosparse -> do continued-train progressive L1 pressure only over FFNs. Limitations: report high dependence over L1 coef (hyperparameter), report only local sparsity (measured over FFN activations); 
- Qsparse -> do train-time top-k clipping over all matmuls (FFNs and attention). Report comparable loss with higher sparsity. Limitation: doesn't evaluate isolated effect of top-k over FFNs and attention;
- Sakana (Sparser, Faster, Lighter Transformer Language Models) -> do train-time l1 pressure on FFNs. Provide kernels and show speedup / performance gains. Limitations: report only local sparsity (over FFNs) and doesn't target attention.

```

| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TEAL — Training-Free Activation Sparsity in LLMs**                       | **Training-free, post-hoc magnitude thresholding** of activations. “Thresholding” is more accurate than *clipping*: small-magnitude activation dimensions are set to zero. TEAL applies this throughout the model, including attention and FFN linear projections, and demonstrates roughly **40–50% model-wide activation sparsity** with limited degradation across Llama-2/3 and Mistral, 7B–70B. ([arXiv][1])                                                                                                                               | It does **not let the model adapt to sparsity during training**; sparsity is imposed on a pretrained representation. Consequently it does not answer whether sparsity-aware training can produce representations that tolerate substantially greater *model-wide* sparsity. It also does not provide a clean causal attention-only vs. FFN-only training comparison.                                                                                |
| **ProSparse**                                                              | Starts from pretrained non-ReLU models, substitutes **ReLU**, performs **continued training**, and progressively increases an \(L_1\) penalty on **FFN intermediate activations**; it finally uses FATReLU/activation-threshold shifting. It reports about **88–89% FFN activation sparsity** for LLaMA2-7B/13B with comparable downstream performance.                                                                                                                                                                                         | The intervention is **FFN-only**. Its headline sparsity is therefore a **local FFN activation statistic**, not the proportion of computation eliminated over the complete Transformer. Sparsity is also controlled indirectly through the regularization coefficient/schedule and final activation threshold, which require tuning. The authors explicitly leave attention sparsification and part of FFN computation for future work. ([arXiv][2]) |
| **Q-Sparse**                                                               | A genuinely **training-time** sparsification intervention: Top-K magnitude sparsification is placed on the input to linear projections in **both attention and FFNs**, with an STE used during backpropagation. It supports training from scratch, continued training, and finetuning. Importantly, Q-Sparse reports **model-wide “overall” sparsity / activated parameters**, in addition to projection-specific QKV, Out, Up, Gate, and Down sparsities. For example, its continued-training models report 45.7% and 58.2% overall sparsity.  | The main quality results **entangle attention and FFN sparsification**. There is no clean `attention only` vs. `FFN only` vs. `attention + FFN` experiment establishing the marginal effect or optimal allocation between these sites. Thus the work establishes that *joint model-wide Top-K training works*, but not **where that sparsity is most valuable**.                                                                                    |
| **Sakana/NVIDIA — Sparser, Faster, Lighter Transformer Language Models**   | Uses **training-time \(L_1\) regularization to induce unstructured activation sparsity in FFNs**, coupled with ReLU-like sparse activations. It shows that very high FFN sparsity—above 95%, and in some settings above 99%—can coexist with little downstream degradation. ([arXiv][3])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Again, the intervention is **FFN-local** and attention remains outside the sparsification target. Reported activation sparsity therefore should not be directly compared with TEAL/Q-Sparse model-wide sparsity. \(L_1\) is also an indirect sparsity-control mechanism, and the main scaling experiments are at roughly 0.5B–2B scale. ([Sakana AI][4])                                                                                                                                                                        | It addresses **allocation**, rather than learning sparsity-aware representations through training. It therefore strengthens the observation that different projections have heterogeneous tolerance, but still does not give the controlled training-time FFN-vs-attention intervention you are interested in.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Its two forms of sparsity are different objects: FFN neuron sparsity versus **token/attention-position sparsity**. It is therefore not equivalent to Q-Sparse/TEAL-style feature sparsification of Q/K/V/O projection inputs, and does not answer whether those attention *linear projections* should themselves be sparsified.                                                                                                                     |

### 2. Inference speedup
The big question is if activation sparsity can map into inference performance gains (e.g. speedups latency / power savings, etc). 

(i) Chain-of-argument:
- Speedup is highly dependent on hardware (GPU) and model architecture / size;
- Published kernels (TEAL and sakana) doesn't extend direct to other models / architectures and require adaptation to specific use cases;
- Specialized kernels require tunning for it for the application and offer a risk if not well-tunned, because a bad kernel adds additional overhead;
- New agentic coding models are extremly good at it and auto-research closed-loop workflows for kernel development and optimization;
- A good kernel found by the agent can benefit from the architecture train-time interventions and higher speedups correlate with higher R_model.

(ii) Extended table with paper synthesis:
```
| Work / paper                             | What the paper actually establishes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Main limitations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TEAL**                                 | This is stronger systems evidence than an isolated-matmul result. TEAL reports **end-to-end single-batch decoding speedups** of up to **1.53× at 40%** and **1.8× at 50% model-wide sparsity**, using sparse kernels that exploit magnitude-pruned activations. ([arXiv][1])                                                                                                                                                                        | The released inference path is explicitly described as a **proof of concept**: FP16 Llama-2/3 support, uniform sparsity, no quantized sparse kernels, and untested speculative decoding. The headline speedup is also for **batch-1 decoding**. Hence it establishes feasibility rather than portability across arbitrary models, dtypes, batches, and GPUs. ([GitHub][2])                                              |
| **ProSparse**                            | Gives two kinds of systems evidence. With **PowerInfer**, the authors report up to **4.52× inference speedup** over their dense llama.cpp setup. They additionally implement predictor-free sparse GPU operators for two FFN steps, obtaining local operator speedups up to **2.44× and 1.70×**.                                                                                                                                                    | The 4.52× result uses PowerInfer's **approximate predictor-based execution**, so it is not purely a sparse-matmul kernel result. Their exact sparse GPU operators accelerate only specific FFN sub-operations. PowerInfer also lacks support for some model/configuration choices used by ProSparse. Thus neither number should be interpreted as a universal model-level speedup from a given FFN sparsity percentage. |
| **Q-Sparse**                             | Establishes that the algorithm reduces **activated parameters/FLOPs**, and introduces **Block Q-Sparse** specifically because naïve Top-K is unfriendly to batched GPU execution; its block structure is designed to map better to hardware-supported N:M-style sparse computation.                                                                                                                                                                 | It does **not close the wall-clock systems loop**. Its efficiency comparisons are primarily based on activated parameters / FLOP-equivalent inference budgets rather than measured end-to-end latency or throughput. This is particularly important because Top-K selection itself has a cost.                                                                                                                          |
| **Sakana/NVIDIA — TwELL**                | Provides some of the strongest direct evidence that unstructured activation sparsity can be converted into practical GPU gains. TwELL introduces a sparse packing representation and fused CUDA kernels for **both inference and training**. On H100, Sakana reports inference improvements reaching roughly **30%** and training improvements up to roughly **24%**, with gains increasing with model scale. ([Sakana AI][3])                      | The implementation is deliberately co-designed for a particular form of **FFN activation sparsity** and modern NVIDIA GPU execution. Those kernels embody assumptions about tensor dimensions, packing, sparsity structure and accelerator behavior; the measured gains should therefore be treated as configuration-specific rather than as a portable sparsity→speedup function.                                      |
| **Spark Transformer**                    | Provides another architecture–systems co-design result: its sparse FFN and sparse-attention architecture reduces FLOPs by about 2.5× and reports up to **1.79× CPU** and **1.40× GPU decoding speedup**. ([arXiv][4])                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | The sparsity pattern is designed together with the architecture, including token-sparse attention. It is evidence that **hardware-aware sparsity design works**, not evidence that arbitrary post-hoc or Q-Sparse-style activation sparsity will inherit the same gains.                                                                                                                                                |
| **KernelBench → CudaForge / CUDA Agent** | Establish the rapid improvement of **agentic kernel optimization**. KernelBench initially showed that frontier models alone rarely beat optimized baselines, while iterative execution/profiling feedback improved performance. By 2025–26, systems such as CudaForge and CUDA Agent show that agents equipped with compilation, correctness testing and hardware profiling can repeatedly discover substantially faster CUDA kernels. ([arXiv][5]) | Most results concern **generic isolated operators**, often against PyTorch or `torch.compile`. They do not imply end-to-end LLM acceleration, and they are not primarily studying TEAL/Q-Sparse-style unstructured activation sparsity.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Agentic Kernel Optimization / Houmao** | Particularly relevant because agents optimize **Fused MoE, Top-K indexing, and sparse attention** on B200, and can outperform specialized FlashInfer baselines. This establishes that agentic optimization is capable of handling irregular/sparse GPU workloads, not only dense GEMMs. ([arXiv][6])                                                                                                                                                | The “sparsity” is primarily **MoE routing and sparse attention**, not sparse feature dimensions entering Transformer linear projections. It therefore demonstrates feasibility of agentic sparse-kernel engineering without solving the activation-sparse GEMM/GEMV problem directly.                                                                                                                                   |
| **GEAK v4**                              | Moves agentic optimization from kernel microbenchmarks to **full vLLM/SGLang workloads**. It profiles the running service, ranks bottlenecks by Amdahl impact, specializes kernels/backends, reintegrates them, and accepts a modification only after end-to-end A/B throughput validation. AMD reports large workload-specific gains, including sparse-attention cases. ([AMD][7])                                                                 | Currently targeted at AMD Instinct systems and particular serving configurations. More importantly, it optimizes whatever bottleneck already exists; it does **not jointly design a train-time activation-sparsification policy and the kernels required to exploit it**.                                                                                                                                               |
