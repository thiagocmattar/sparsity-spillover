\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Activation sparsity can improve language-model inference efficiency when specialized kernels skip computations associated with zero activations. Recent work has shown that sparsity can be exposed in pretrained models through post-hoc thresholding or induced during training, in both cases with measured inference speedups~\citep{liu2024teal,cetin2026sparser}. These results raise two practical design questions: where should zeros be exposed and, independently, where should sparsity pressure be applied? Which resulting zero structure can kernels exploit?

Existing approaches induce activation sparsity in different ways. TEAL applies post-hoc thresholding to pretrained models, ProSparse uses L1 regularization on feed-forward (FFN) activations during training, and Q-Sparse applies top-k sparsification to both FFN and attention activations~\citep{liu2024teal,song2024prosparse,wang2024qsparse}. While each approach is effective in its own setting, it remains unclear how they compare under a common setup and how their effects interact when combined.% An intervention that works well in isolation may add little on top of another or incur a different quality cost when applied at additional sites, making sparsity alone insufficient to identify which choices drive the improvement.

In this work, we study the effects of (i) activation L1 pressure and (ii) \emph{thresholding}, which maps small activations exactly to zero. Pressure encourages smaller activation magnitudes; thresholding converts small values into exact zeros. Their placement defines separate design choices. We evaluate their individual and combined effects at different sites within the same transformer block.

To measure their effects, rather than report sparsity locally at individual activation sites, we define model-wide activation sparsity, $\Smodel$, as the fraction of forward matrix-multiplication products with a zero activation operand. We additionally derive an architecture-dependent maximum sparsity that can be achieved through the intervention set ($\Smax$).

We pretrain Pythia-family models from scratch for controlled comparisons across model sizes~\citep{biderman2023pythia}. Across 40 conditions at 14M, we separate threshold placement from pressure placement. Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview} compares the same six recipe families at 14M and 70M, using 22 trained checkpoints at each size alongside post-hoc clipping of the controls. Narrow pressure at the FFN hidden site gives favorable moderate-threshold trade-offs at 14M. At the highest threshold, expanding seven-site pressure from $h$ to all selected sites increases sparsity at both sizes, but raises loss at 14M and lowers it at 70M. The more sparse 70M model nevertheless has higher optimized latency. These results distinguish the quality cost of inducing zeros from their value to the execution kernel.
