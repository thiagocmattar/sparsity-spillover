\subsection{From sparsity to speedups}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

\textbf{Agentic kernel development.}
We used Codex with GPT-6 Astra~\citep{openaigpt6astra} to develop specialized GPU kernels under human guidance, starting from the implementation of \citet{cetin2026sparser} and adapting it to Pythia. The agent proposed and tested changes on a fixed subset of training sequences, comparing outputs with the unmodified model. Each selected implementation was frozen before evaluation on the final checkpoints. We checked outputs on the full validation set against declared numerical tolerances, and timed a fixed subset of sequences. The 14M kernel and its initial 70M port were tested across their respective grids; the subsequent 70M optimization covered two checkpoints. Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results} specifies the execution paths, correctness checks and timing protocol. This case illustrates how agentic coding can make direct execution tests practical for a representation-learning study.

\textbf{More sparsity need not mean lower latency.}
Within the tested recipes, higher sparsity generally accompanies lower latency, but placement matters. At 14M and $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ is much sparser than $T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ ($27.48\%$ versus $12.71\%$), yet slower (0.473 versus 0.459\,ms; Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}). Figure~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency} further shows that reducing latency within the transferred kernel need not beat native PyTorch. The benefit depends on where zeros occur, whether they form skippable groups, and the cost of detecting them. Section~\ref{sec:kernel-autoresearch} isolates these mechanisms.

\textbf{Post-hoc zeros alone do not ensure acceleration.}
The retained clipping measurements reach at most $1.025\times$ the native base-model speed at 14M and $0.613\times$ at 70M (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results}). The small 14M difference spans GPU sessions and does not establish a reliable clipping benefit. Our implementation adds a separate masking pass, and the resulting zeros must still form groups the kernel can skip. We did not optimize fused clipping, so these results describe this implementation rather than a general limit of post-hoc sparsification.

\subsection{Paired effects of pressure placement}
\label{sec:paired-results}

% Source: analyses/024-2026-09-17-h-only-kernel-latency/observations/015-pressure-scope-caption.md;
% generating analysis: paper_effect_figures.py / 13_rebuild_paper_figures.py;
% adoption and checked claims: manuscript/draft/reviews/2026-09-18-paired-pressure/README.md.
One question raised during sparse intervetion design is wheater activation pressure should be applied over the entire network, or are there local sites more suceptible to sparsification? To answer this, we combine the clipping accross multiple sites ($T_4$ and $T_7$) with global and local FFN hidden pressure ($P_{all}$ and $P_h$).

We find that \textbf{local h-only pressure gives a more favorable quality--sparsity trade-off at moderate thresholds in both model sizes}.
For $\kappa\leq0.1$, $P_h$ lowers loss relative to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ in all 16 matched four-/seven-site comparisons at 14M and 70M
(Figure~\ref{fig:paired-intervention-effects}). The loss reduction is
$0.19$--$0.32$, with sparsity differing by at most $3.36$ percentage points.
At $\kappa=0.5$, all-site pressure instead gives substantially more sparsity,
especially for $T_7$; at 70M it also lowers the $T_7$ loss by $0.121$.

H-only pressure has lower measured latency in 18 of the 20 pairs, including
all ten 70M pairs. The two small reversals occur at 14M and $\kappa=0.5$:
all-site pressure is faster by $2.78\,\mu$s for $T_4$ and $0.52\,\mu$s for
$T_7$. Thus, the extra sparsity from broader pressure does not consistently
translate into lower latency. Detailed 14M pressure-budget diagnostics are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}.

Additionally, \textbf{local pressure can produce nonlocal sparsity changes}.
At $\kappa=0.05$ in 14M, $P_h$ increases sparsity at both $h$ and $z$
relative to $P_0$ under $T_4$ and $T_7$, although $z$ receives no direct
pressure (Figure~\ref{fig:pressure-layer-sparsity}). Under $T_7$, $P_h$ also
produces higher sparsity at $h$ and $z$ than $P_{\mathrm{all}}$, despite
the latter targeting both sites. More frequent activation of the OL1 norm
cap under $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ offers a possible explanation
(Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}), but these diagnostics do
not isolate its contribution. At $\kappa=0.5$, $h$ and $z$ are nearly
fully sparse under every pressure scope; the larger differences instead
appear at $q,k,v$.
