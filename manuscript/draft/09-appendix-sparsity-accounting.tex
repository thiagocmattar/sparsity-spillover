\subsection{Activation and model-wide sparsity}

Activation sparsity is the fraction of zero entries at a site, pooling
counts across layers and validation sequences before dividing.

Model-wide sparsity counts scalar multiplications with a zero activation input:
\begin{equation}
\Smodel=\frac{N_0}{N_{\mathrm{model}}}.
\end{equation}
$N_0$ counts these multiplications within Pythia blocks;
$N_{\mathrm{model}}$ counts all multiplications in those blocks and the
final vocabulary projection. The latter contributes only to the denominator.
Counts are pooled across layers and validation sequences; percentages are
$100\Smodel$.

For a block's linear projection from $d_{\mathrm{in}}$ to
$d_{\mathrm{out}}$ features over $n$ rows, the denominator contribution is
$n d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}$. Each zero input contributes
$d_{\mathrm{out}}$ to $N_0$, one multiplication per output feature.
Zero weights do not count as activation sparsity.

In attention, both operands are activations. We count each multiplication
once if either or both are zero. For queries $q$, keys $k$, values $v$ and
attention probabilities $p$, one head in one sequence contributes
\begin{align}
N_{0,\mathrm{QK}}&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{u=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{d_h}
\mathbf{1}\{q_{tj}=0\ \lor\ k_{uj}=0\},\label{eq:qk-count}\\
N_{0,\mathrm{PV}}&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{u=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{d_h}
\mathbf{1}\{p_{tu}=0\ \lor\ v_{uj}=0\}.\label{eq:pv-count}
\end{align}
Here $T$ is sequence length, $d_h$ the head dimension, $t$ the query position
and $u$ the key/value position. Restricting $u\leq t$ excludes masked future
positions from both counts. We sum over heads, layers and validation sequences,
using the actual operands after thresholding and intervening transformations.

Normalization, softmax, activation functions, additions and memory transfers
are excluded, so $\Smodel$ does not directly measure latency or speedup.

\subsection{Model-wide sparsity ceiling}
\label{app:ceiling}

Assume all activations at the thresholding sites $\mathcal{N}$ are zero.
If $N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})$ counts the multiplications that must
then have a zero activation input, the ceiling is
\begin{equation}
\Smax=\frac{N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})}{N_{\mathrm{model}}}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is unchanged, and each multiplication is counted once.
The numerator includes downstream activations forced to zero: setting
$v=0$ also makes $z=0$, affecting both $PV$ and the attention-output projection.
Zero attention scores do not imply zero softmax probabilities.

The ceiling depends only on architecture, selected sites and sequence length.
Natural zeros outside those sites can raise measured $\Smodel$ above it;
the ceiling does not predict the associated quality cost.
Appendix~\ref{app:architecture-counts} gives the counts for each Pythia size.
