\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Where zeros are exposed need not equal where sparsity pressure is applied.
In our 14M sweep, threshold scope and pressure scope define distinct operating
regimes: h-only pressure is favorable at moderate thresholds, whereas broad
seven-site pressure exposes the highest sparsity at $\kappa=0.5$. Holding
h-only pressure fixed gives a cleaner threshold-placement comparison, with
the broader threshold scope adding $6.44$ pp sparsity for $+0.0094$ loss.

These observations suggest a scoped rule of thumb: in our 14M sweep, use
threshold placement to expose computational reach; start pressure narrowly
at the FFN hidden site when quality preservation matters, and broaden it
when the target operating point requires extreme sparsity. This is not yet
a recommendation across model sizes. In the 70M extension, broadening
seven-site pressure at the highest threshold increases sparsity and lowers
loss relative to h-only pressure, reversing the 14M quality cost. The
410M cohort establishes only the broad-pressure recipe ordering.

Product accounting and kernel timing answer different questions.
$\Smodel$ measures how much matrix-product work is logically exposed to zero
operands; the kernel study asks how much of that opportunity can be
profitably realized. The distinction matters across sites: high-threshold
projection sparsity produces structured work that K050 can skip with a net
latency benefit, while additional Q/K/V sparsity increases the logical
opportunity without producing profitable attention skipping at the tested
14M shapes. The computational value of a sparse representation therefore
depends not only on how many zeros it contains, but also on where they occur
and the granularity at which the implementation can exploit them.

Cross-size comparisons use a common token budget rather than equal tokens
per parameter, and 410M also uses a lower peak learning rate; the A0
optimization trajectories in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization}
show that the three sizes finish in different realized optimization regimes.
Accordingly, the scale experiment tests recurrence of within-size recipe
relationships rather than quality scaling with parameter count.
These conclusions concern MiniPile pretraining. The distribution diagnostics cover 14M and describe responses
rather than isolating a causal pathway for the nonlocal response. The kernel results use RTX5090 hardware,
full-sequence inference, native SDPA and matched fusion controls; Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview}
adds 70M execution and post-hoc clipping measurements from separate physical
GPU/host sessions. Transfer to cached decoding, 410M execution and other
hardware remains untested. The 70M port is shape-specific; the two sizes do
not constitute an equal-budget kernel optimization comparison.

The pressure-scope controls sharpen identification but do not complete it.
Each condition has one training seed, and the shared validation set is not
an independent confirmation set. Because $T_7/P_4$ is absent, the
$P_h\rightarrow P_7$ contrast jointly changes pressure at $a,m,z,q,k,v$
and the objective normalization; it cannot isolate Q/K/V pressure.
The 70M h-only comparison lacks multisite no-pressure controls, and h-only
training remains untested at 410M. The new full-model timings in
Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview} do not extend the historical
skipping-ablation cohort. Clipping latency includes recurring mask operators
and uses a separate session, limiting small cross-session comparisons.
