\subsection{Quality--sparsity operating regimes}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

\textbf{The interventions extend the attainable sparsity range, sometimes
close to the architectural ceiling, at a quality cost.}
Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-14m-70m} summarizes 58 trained
conditions at 14M and 70M. At $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches
$27.48\%$ and $40.60\%$ model-wide sparsity: $91.8\%$ and $82.2\%$
of the respective seven-site ceilings. Validation loss increases by
$0.62$ and $1.12$ relative to the same-size base model.
The complete 410M stress-test panel is retained in
Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-410m}, alongside the full
post-hoc trajectories in Figure~\ref{fig:clipping-410m} and the endpoint tables.

\textbf{Agentic kernel development.}
To test whether learned sparsity reduces inference latency, we used Codex,
configured with GPT-6 Astra~\citep{openaigpt6astra}, to develop specialized
GPU kernels under human guidance. Starting from the kernels released by
\citet{cetin2026sparser}, the agent proposed code changes and tested their
speed and accuracy. During
development, we tested changes on the same small set of training text
sequences, comparing the model's output scores and loss with and without
the custom GPU code. We specified how much these values were allowed to
differ and rejected versions that exceeded those limits. After selecting
the kernels for 14M, we stopped changing their code and tested the model saved
at the end of training for each recipe. For each
model, we measured inference time on the same 64 validation sequences
and checked output agreement on all 338 complete validation sequences;
its speedup was included only if these checks passed. We repeated this
evaluation at 70M after adapting the kernels to its larger matrix dimensions
(Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results}). For our representation-learning team,
which lacks specialist GPU-kernel expertise, this workflow made direct
execution tests practical. This case illustrates how coding agents can
support systems development.

GPUs multiply blocks of values, called tiles, so isolated zeros need not
save computation. Our projection kernels skip matrix operations for entirely
zero activation tiles: $16\times16$ at $a,m$ and $8\times16$ at $h,z$
(tokens $\times$ features). At $h,z$, checking tiles before loading weights
also avoids the corresponding memory reads; nearly empty rows can instead
use only their nonzero values. Speedup therefore depends on how zeros
cluster, which operations exploit them, and the cost of detecting them.

\textbf{Sparsity and speedup rise together within recipes.}
Figure~\ref{fig:base-model-speedup} shows an approximately linear increase
within each trained threshold/pressure family over the tested grid, with
different slopes across families. At 14M and $\kappa=0.5$, for example,
$T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ is much sparser than $T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$
($27.48\%$ versus $12.71\%$), yet slightly slower ($1.38\times$ versus
$1.42\times$ base-model speedup). Thus, a larger $\Smodel$ need not yield a
larger gain: where the zeros occur and whether the kernel can exploit them
matter (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-autoresearch}).

\textbf{Post-hoc clipping does not yield comparable gains in this implementation.}
Every positive clipping target remains below $1\times$ at 14M. At 70M,
aggressive clipping can exceed $1\times$, reaching $1.22\times$, but with
substantial loss degradation (Appendix~\ref{app:clipping-results}).
Here clipping uses separate masking operations around the frozen kernel,
adding activation scans and writes on every forward pass. The resulting zeros
still have to form groups that its skipping rules can use. These costs can
outweigh the avoided products; the measurements do not separately isolate
each cost. We did not optimize a fused clipping implementation, so this
result does not establish a general limitation of post-hoc sparsification.

\subsection{Paired effects of pressure placement}
\label{sec:paired-results}

% Source: analyses/024-2026-09-17-h-only-kernel-latency/observations/015-pressure-scope-caption.md;
% generating analysis: paper_effect_figures.py / 13_rebuild_paper_figures.py;
% adoption and checked claims: manuscript/draft/reviews/2026-09-18-paired-pressure/README.md.
\textbf{H-only pressure gives a more favorable quality--sparsity trade-off
at moderate thresholds in both model sizes.}
For $\kappa\leq0.1$, $P_h$ lowers loss relative to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ in
all 16 matched four-/seven-site comparisons at 14M and 70M
(Figure~\ref{fig:paired-intervention-effects}). The loss reduction is
$0.19$--$0.32$, with sparsity differing by at most $3.36$ percentage points.
At $\kappa=0.5$, all-site pressure instead gives substantially more sparsity,
especially for $T_7$; at 70M it also lowers the $T_7$ loss by $0.121$.

H-only pressure has lower measured latency in 18 of the 20 pairs, including
all ten 70M pairs. The two small reversals occur at 14M and $\kappa=0.5$:
all-site pressure is faster by $2.78\,\mu$s for $T_4$ and $0.52\,\mu$s for
$T_7$. Thus, the extra sparsity from broader pressure does not consistently
translate into lower latency. Detailed 14M pressure-budget diagnostics are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}.
