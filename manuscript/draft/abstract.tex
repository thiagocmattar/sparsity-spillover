\begin{abstract}
Activation sparsity can accelerate language-model inference when specialized kernels skip computation associated with zero activations. Existing methods obtain sparsity through different combinations of training-time pressure ($P$) and thresholding ($T$), but their relative and combined effects have not been isolated under a common setup. We study these interventions under matched pretraining conditions across Pythia 14M and 70M models. For each combination of $P$ and $T$, we derive an architecture-dependent maximum achievable sparsity ($\Smax$), measure model-wide attained activation sparsity ($\Smodel$) and the final inference latency under a specialized sparse kernel.
Results points to four different findings. First, \textbf{aggressive combinations of interventions can extend the attainable sparsity range}. The higher-thresholding ($\kappa=0.5$) broader-site pressure, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$, reaches $27.5\%$ and $40.6\%$ model-wide sparsity at 14M and 70M, respectively $92\%$ and $82\%$ of the architectural ceilings, but at the validation loss cost of $0.62$ and $1.12$. 
Secondly, \textbf{sparsity and speedup increase together within recipes}. Although they correlate, not all sparsity translates into speedups. Exploiting it requires specialized kernel development: 70M required a further optimization step because the 14M implementation did not transfer efficiently. 
For example, the sparser 14M recipe $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ achieves $27.48\%$ sparsity but is slower than $T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ at $12.71\%$ ($0.473$ versus $0.459$\,ms).
Third, we \textbf{observe a phenomenon of sparsity spillover}: local pressure can causa a non-local response. At 14M, FFN-hidden ($h$) pressure increases sparsity at the unpressured attention-output site ($z$) by $10.7$ percentage points relative to no pressure, and \textbf{the biggest latency gains come from bypassing them}. Also, pressuring $h$ alone is less harmurful to validation loss than global pressure. We hypothese that some transformer sites are more succeptible to sparsification than others.
Our main takeaway is that training interventions and deployment kernels must be co-designed so that the induced sparsity is both high and structurally exploitable at inference time.
\end{abstract}
