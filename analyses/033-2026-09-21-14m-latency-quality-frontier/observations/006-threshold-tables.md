# Aggressive and moderate 14M intervention tables

Question: How broadly and aggressively should interventions be applied to
reduce latency at an acceptable quality cost?

The user requested two manuscript tables: all seven T2/T4/T7 recipes at
kappa=0.5, and the same seven recipes at kappa=0.05 and 0.1. Base is repeated
as a reference. T1 varies pressure weight rather than kappa, so it is outside
these matched-threshold tables. No new measurements are made.

Source: [frontier.json](../data/frontier.json), generated by `01_plot.py`,
SHA256 `9402ea4238c95683230c11c3a5ca93531efb1bfe6bc163663c3931eea6cb1f32`.
The hand-edited tables in `manuscript/draft/training-results.tex` use each
selected record's ordinary full-validation loss, pooled model-wide sparsity
and retained K050 latency. Ceilings (5.35%, 12.83%, 29.95%) match the manuscript
recipe table; they denote analytic reach, not measured activation zero rates.
Each table has Recipe, Ceiling (%), Sparsity (%), Latency (ms), and Loss;
the moderate table adds kappa, with paired rows grouped by recipe.

Coverage and timing follow observation 001: 338 full-validation blocks,
1,444-token excluded tail; RTX5090, BF16, batch one, 2,048 tokens, full logits,
64 sequences x seven timing passes x three processes. Base is explicitly
the kernel reference (0.651573 ms), not native PyTorch. Historical timing
sessions are retained without normalization. The tables contain all 21
eligible intervention checkpoints plus the repeated Base reference.

At kappa=0.5, T7/Pall reaches 27.48% sparsity versus T4/Pall's 12.71%, but
takes 0.473 versus 0.459 ms. Within T7, Pall adds 10.82 sparsity percentage
points relative to Ph, while latency differs by only 0.52 microseconds and
loss increases from 5.732 to 5.829. All seven aggressive endpoints are faster
than the kernel Base and have higher loss. This supports the absence of a
consistent latency benefit from broader coverage, not a claim that breadth
never helps.

At both moderate thresholds, Ph has lower loss and latency than Pall under
both T4 and T7 (four comparisons). At kappa=0.05, T4/Ph achieves loss 5.196
and 0.562 ms, while T2/Ph has lower loss (5.134) but higher latency (0.590 ms).
The interpretation therefore identifies a trade-off rather than claiming a
single universally best recipe. Comparisons remain descriptive and single-seed;
small timing differences are not significance results or causal allocations.
