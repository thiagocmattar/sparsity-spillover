\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Sparsification interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}

We study fixed elementwise thresholds that leave the number of surviving
activations free to vary with the learned representation. Unlike top-$k$
selection or its statistical approximation~\citep{wang2024qsparse,you2025spark},
a fixed cutoff does not target a prescribed activation count. It requires
neither ranking nor input-dependent moment estimates. This motivates the
design choice; an end-to-end speed advantage over selection-based methods
would require a separate implementation comparison.

A sparsification intervention specifies thresholding sites $\mathcal{N}$,
their nonlinearities and thresholds, and pressure sites $\mathcal{P}$ at which
training encourages smaller activation magnitudes. Choosing where to apply a
threshold and where to apply pressure are separate decisions, although many
evaluated recipes align the two sets. Figure~\ref{fig:intervention-ladder}
gives the assignments; the cohort does not cross every possible combination.

We use ReLU and two thresholding nonlinearities:
$G_{+,\kappa}(x)=x\mathbf{1}\{x\geq\kappa\}$ and
$G_{\pm,\kappa}(x)=x\mathbf{1}\{|x|\geq\kappa\}$, with $\kappa\geq0$.
One-sided thresholding removes all negative values and positive values below $\kappa$;
symmetric thresholding removes small magnitudes while preserving the signs
and values of survivors. At $\kappa=0$, their forward maps are ReLU and the
identity, respectively. Appendix~\ref{app:interventions} specifies boundary
and derivative conventions.

The threshold is fixed within each training condition. We hypothesize that
training can reshape the activation distribution around this nonlinearity,
with pressure providing an additional influence. We examine quality and
sparsity at the final checkpoints and compare their signed activation
distributions. These endpoint measurements describe the resulting
representations; they do not identify the adaptation process during training.

Pressure uses $\mathcal{L}_1$: we take the mean absolute activation in each
targeted site--layer tensor, after the nonlinearity where one is applied, then average these
means equally. Adding target sites therefore changes the objective and reduces
the weight of each existing site--layer term. Equal $\lambda$ across target
sets does not mean equal per-site pressure.

\emph{Naive L1} optimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_1$,
clipping the combined gradient before AdamW.
\emph{Orthogonal L1} (OL1) adapts gradient-conflict
projection~\citep{yu2020pcgrad,hsieh2024bloop} to this pressure objective.
It makes a task-only AdamW update, then adds a pressure
correction preconditioned by the task's second-moment estimates. When this
correction opposes the adaptive task direction, OL1 projects out its
conflicting component. A positive trust budget caps its norm relative to that task
direction. This limits the pressure correction without guaranteeing preserved task
loss; Appendix~\ref{app:pressure} defines the procedure.

\subsection{Sparsity and architectural reach}
\label{sec:measurement}

\emph{Activation sparsity} is the fraction of exactly zero entries at a named site.
We weight zeros by their affected work using \emph{model-wide activation sparsity},
$\Smodel=N_0/N_{\mathrm{model}}$, reported as $100\Smodel\%$.
One scalar multiplication in a declared transformer-block matrix product
receives one zero credit if either activation operand is zero, including when
both are zero. The denominator also includes the dense language-model (LM)
head, which receives no zero credit. We pool integer counts before dividing
and count only causally valid pairs in full, uncached sequences.
Appendix~\ref{app:accounting} specifies the operations and actual operands.
This fraction measures a declared multiplication opportunity, not total
FLOPs, memory traffic or elapsed time.

The \emph{architectural reach},
$\Rarch=N_{\mathrm{reach}}(\mathcal{N})/N_{\mathrm{model}}$, is the fraction
of the declared workload structurally reached when the selected sites are
entirely zero, counting overlapping effects once. It depends on the
architecture, site set and sequence length, but not on a checkpoint, pressure
objective or quality constraint. Observed $\Smodel$ also includes natural
zeros outside the selected-site reach, so $\Rarch$ need not bound every
observation. Its gap to measured sparsity does not quantify further sparsity
available at acceptable quality. Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling} defines the
reach accounting.
