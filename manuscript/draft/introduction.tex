Activation sparsity can improve language-model inference efficiency when specialized kernels skip computation and memory access associated with zero activations. Recent work has shown that sparsity can be exposed in pretrained models through post-hoc thresholding or induced during training, in both cases with measured inference speedups~\citep{liu2024teal,cetin2026sparser}. These results raise a practical design question: how should a researcher place and combine sparsification interventions to obtain more useful sparsity at an acceptable quality cost? Answering this question matters when choosing which components of a recipe to retain, where to invest additional training, and which operations justify sparse-kernel development.

Existing approaches induce activation sparsity in different ways. TEAL applies post-hoc thresholding to pretrained models, ProSparse uses L1 regularization on FFN activations during training, and Q-Sparse applies top-k sparsification to both FFN and attention activations~\citep{liu2024teal,song2024prosparse,wang2024qsparse}. While each approach is effective in its own setting, it remains unclear how they compare under a common setup and how their effects interact when combined. An intervention that works well in isolation may add little on top of another or incur a different quality cost when applied at additional sites, making sparsity alone insufficient to identify which choices drive the improvement.

In this work, we study three sparsification interventions: (i) \emph{pressure}, an optimization regularizer that forces selected activations toward zero; (ii) \emph{thresholding}, a nonlinear activation function that maps small activations exactly to zero; and (iii) \emph{placement}, which determines where these interventions are applied. Under matched pretraining conditions, this paired design isolates the individual and interaction effects of each intervention and connects them to the amount of model-wide computation exposed to sparsification. Rather than report sparsity locally at individual activation sites, we define model-wide activation sparsity, $\Smodel$, as the fraction of forward matrix-multiplication products with a zero activation operand. We additionally derive an architecture-dependent sparsity ceiling, $\Smax$, corresponding to the maximum sparsity that can be achieved through the intervention set. Together, $\Smodel$ and $\Smax$ quantify the achieved and maximum fraction of model-wide matrix-multiplication work exposed to sparsification.

We pretrain Pythia-family models from scratch at multiple scales to study sparsification under controlled conditions~\citep{biderman2023pythia}. At 14M, we evaluate 30 training conditions across eight recipes, yielding 29 paired comparisons of pressure, thresholding, and placement. Broader thresholding produces substantially more model-wide sparsity than local FFN pressure, while combining thresholding with optimization pressure yields the highest sparsity. At the largest tested threshold, seven-site thresholding reaches $15.39\%$ model-wide sparsity; adding orthogonal pressure raises it to $27.48\%$ against a $29.95\%$ architecture-dependent ceiling. We scale selected combinations to 70M and 410M, where seven-site thresholding with pressure reaches $40.60\%$ and $80.62\%$ sparsity against ceilings of $49.42\%$ and $87.25\%$. Across all three sizes, trained recipes outperform uniform post-hoc thresholding in the aggressive-clipping region of the quality--sparsity frontier. Activation-distribution analyses further show that broader placement increases model-wide sparsity despite producing fewer FFN zeros because it also creates exact zeros in attention operands.

Finally, we test whether specialized kernels can translate these sparsity gains into faster inference using GPT-6-Astra. Across 42 eligible development iterations, the selected implementation achieves a $1.78\times$ full-model speedup over native PyTorch/SDPA on an RTX5090 for Pythia-14M at $27.48\%$ model-wide sparsity, near its $29.95\%$ ceiling. Across all 30 14M interventions, speedup is strongly and approximately linearly associated with $\Smodel$ ($R^2=0.817$), showing that model-wide sparsity predicts realized speedup in this workload. Ablations show that both fusion and sparse execution contribute to the gains, while disabling attention skipping improves every checkpoint. The attention kernel can skip multiplication when either operand fragment is entirely zero, but detection and control overhead exceed the saved computation at this model size. Realizing speedups from sparsity therefore requires matching sparse-execution granularity and overhead to the available zero structure.
