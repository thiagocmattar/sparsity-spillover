\subsection{Realized training protocol}
\label{app:training-protocol}

We use MiniPile and the Pythia-14M-deduped tokenizer for every size. Each document
is encoded without added special tokens, followed by one end-of-sequence token.
Concatenated tokens are packed into nonoverlapping 2,048-token blocks. Training
uses a seeded permutation of the block indices; validation retains source
order. The training split contains 1,000,000 documents and 1,491,711,416 tokens,
yielding 728,374 complete blocks and an excluded 1,464-token tail.
The supplementary \texttt{protocol.json} records immutable dataset and tokenizer
revisions, cache hashes and implementation identities.

All conditions start from random weights drawn from the pinned Pythia
architecture configurations; released pretrained weights are not used.
Model initialization and data-order seeds are both 1234, with one training
seed per condition. Parameter sweeps measure intervention variability rather
than variation over independent training seeds. All quality comparisons reuse
the same validation split, without a separate confirmation set. Within a size, the
conditions share the initial parameter hash and training schedule. The
30-condition 14M cohort contains A0, A1-H, four weights each for A1-H-L1 and
A1-H-OL1, and five thresholds each for A4, A4-OL1, A7 and A7-OL1. Each
larger cohort contains A0, A1-H, and the five-threshold A4-OL1/A7-OL1 sweeps.
Local pressure weights are $\{0.05,0.1,0.5,1\}$; thresholds are
$\{0,0.01,0.05,0.1,0.5\}$. Multisite OL1 uses $\lambda=1$, with pressure
at all four or seven selected sites. Every OL1 condition uses trust budget 1
and projection stabilizer $\epsilon=10^{-12}$.

Training uses AdamW with $\beta=(0.9,0.95)$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, weight
decay 0.1 and global task-gradient clipping at norm 1. Naive L1 clips the
combined objective gradient instead. The learning rate warms up linearly
for eight updates and follows cosine decay to one tenth of its peak.
Parameters and optimizer state are FP32; forward computation uses FP16
autocast with dynamic loss scaling. Attention and hidden dropout are zero.
Table~\ref{tab:training-settings} gives size-dependent settings. The global
batch is 1,024 sequences of 2,048 tokens, for 712 optimizer updates and
1,493,172,224 input tokens. This wraps 714 blocks beyond the 728,374 complete
blocks in one packed training pass. We use the final checkpoint without
validation-based checkpoint selection. The lower 410M learning rate is part
of the realized protocol, so size alone is not the only cross-scale change.

Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization} shows the A0 optimization trajectories under
the common token budget. Training loss trends downward across the runs,
but the three sizes finish in different optimization regimes: 70M reaches
the lowest training loss under the realized schedule, while 410M finishes
above it and exhibits a larger pre-clipping task-gradient norm late in
training. These observations are \textbf{consistent with different degrees
of optimization under the fixed-token protocol}, but they do not establish
whether any run has or has not converged. The trajectories therefore provide
context for the non-monotonic A0 validation losses in
Figure~\ref{fig:scale-transfer} rather than a basis for extrapolating
performance with additional training.

\subsection{Activation sparsity and signed-density coverage}
\label{app:density-results}

The seven frozen-checkpoint evaluations in
Figure~\ref{fig:activation-density-full} record signed values after the trained
nonlinearities, with Q/K measured after RoPE. Each checkpoint contributes
2,658,140,160 FFN elements and 1,594,884,096 attention elements across the
complete validation set. Summing integer counts gives the 80:20 weighting
of $h:m$ and equal weighting of $q:k:v$. These pooled distributions are
element-weighted mixtures, not distributions of layer means.

Exact zeros are stored separately from the nonzero histogram. The native
grid spans $[-8,8]$ with 16,000 bins of nominal width 0.001; the figure
combines ten bins at a time and divides by the actual floating-point bin
width and the count of all captured elements. Consequently, a curve's full
integral is the recorded nonzero mass inside that grid, not one. Table~\ref{tab:density-mass}
reports the zero mass and the fractions outside the displayed and native
ranges. Underflow and overflow counts are retained rather than folded into
edge bins. The displayed limits are $[-2.5,3]$ for FFN and $[-4,4]$ for
attention in Figure~\ref{fig:activation-density-full}. The largest omitted
display tail is $6.6719\%$; the native-grid tail is at most $0.7607\%$.
Figure~\ref{fig:activation-reshaping} selects the two OL1 checkpoints at
$\kappa=0.5$, using the same density normalization and symlog scale, with
FFN limits $[0,3]$ and attention limits $[-4,4]$.

The diagnostic reruns use FP32 parameters with FP16 autocast and no
additional clipping. Their maximum absolute validation-loss difference
from the retained reference is $6.89\times10^{-5}$, below the specified
$5\times10^{-4}$ tolerance; all 108 threshold-consistency checks pass. Small
rounding differences in exact-zero counts between evaluation passes do not
change the displayed conclusions. Complete per-site and pooled histograms,
counts and checkpoint hashes accompany the data release.

\subsection{Kernel qualification and ablations}
\label{app:kernel-results}

The unchanged Sakana/TwELL control achieves $1.3001\times$ on its supported
SparseLM-0.5B/H100 workload. Its raw projection primitive at the Pythia-14M
width $N=128$ instead gives relative L2 error 0.69055, exceeding the 0.02
bound; that test stops before full-model Pythia timing. This is a compatibility
test, not an unchanged upstream speedup comparison on our model.
The later P0 adapter changes signed-value packing, shape tails and numerical
handling for Pythia. It retains dense attention and uses native projection
fallback on A0. Table~\ref{tab:kernel-ablations} reports this adapted baseline.

Historical proposals are replayed on four representative checkpoints; the
incumbent in Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-search-history} uses fixed A7-OL1,
$\kappa=0.5$. It reaches $1.6024\times$ by iteration 11 and $1.7830\times$
at iteration 42. Final implementation and
ablation summaries retain the 30-checkpoint manuscript cohort from the
historical 35-checkpoint sweep. Five A4 checkpoints with pressure only at $h$
are excluded because they do not implement the four-site pressure objective
studied here. Search progress and the
final sweep are separate measurements, explaining the small difference
between their $1.7830\times$ and $1.7832\times$ maxima.

The coding-agent configuration records \emph{gpt-6-astra} as the default
model, not the model served for every request. This is one human-guided
development trajectory and does not compare agent-assisted with human-only
kernel development. Kernel measurements use Python 3.12.3, PyTorch
2.11.0+cu128, Transformers 5.12.1, CUDA 12.8 and nvcc 12.8.93.
The supplementary protocol records dependency and frozen-source hashes.

Every candidate must produce finite outputs, with each logit satisfying
$|y-\hat y|\leq0.25+0.02|\hat y|$, per-block relative output L2 error at
most 0.02, and absolute pooled loss difference at most 0.001. Here
$\hat y$ is the unmodified native eager reference. All three fresh
processes must pass on all 338 validation blocks. Qualification is bounded
numerical agreement on these inputs, not bitwise equivalence. Failed
candidates are excluded from qualified speedup summaries. P0, an early
projection adapter derived from the released kernels of
\citet{cetin2026sparser}, qualifies on only four checkpoints. Its average
is not comparable to a 30-checkpoint mean or an unchanged upstream benchmark.

Timing uses the same checkpoint, inputs and CUDA-graph scaffold for native
PyTorch/SDPA and each candidate. For each checkpoint, we take the geometric
mean of 1,344 paired host-latency ratios (native divided by candidate): 64 inputs,
seven randomized paired passes, and three fresh processes. Compilation,
static weight preparation, graph capture and equal input staging
are excluded. Preprocessing that depends on activations, embeddings, every block,
final normalization and full vocabulary logits are included. There is no
activation cache. Cohort summaries give each checkpoint equal weight.

K050 combines normalization and RoPE fusion, zero-fragment tensor-core
bypass and sparse-row projection paths, using CUDA, CUTLASS,
FlashAttention and PyTorch components. K049 is its searched predecessor;
K050 adds fusion of the two input LayerNorms while preserving their separate
affine parameters and thresholds. The all-skips-off control retains
fusion while disabling both attention and projection skips; the
attention-dense control retains projection skips. The historical ablation
summary forms ratios from separately measured, native-normalized timings.
Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-autoresearch}b instead directly divides candidate
latency geometric means, as defined below. K050's sparse paths
improve 14 of 30 checkpoints relative to all-skips-off, with geometric
mean ratio $1.0432\times$. Disabling attention skipping improves all 30:
at the search checkpoint, instrumentation confirms that skipping bypasses
$58.0\%$ of eligible $QK$ and $67.2\%$ of $PV$ tensor-core
matrix multiply-accumulate (MMA) instructions, yet its overhead
exceeds the saved time. Scalar zero-product fractions and skipped MMA
fractions are different estimands.

The attention path tests whether either warp-distributed operand fragment
contains only zeros, and issues an MMA only when both fragments contain a
nonzero. Partial sparsity can therefore leave an entire instruction active.
Zero detection, warp votes and branches recur inside the kernel; causal
masking and softmax still execute. A zero $QK$ score does not imply a zero
softmax probability. The measurements do not identify a profitable joint-zero
pattern or break-even threshold. They show that the skipped work does not
repay these costs for the measured 14M shapes and hardware.

\subsection{Structured sparsity and matched runtime diagnostics}
\label{app:kernel-diagnostics}

We reconcile the same 30 checkpoints with their canonical logical-product
counts, retained kernel diagnostics, and raw matched timing pairs.
Table~\ref{tab:kernel-checkpoint-latencies} separates native and optimized
latencies for each checkpoint. Let $t_N,t_0,t_P,t_K$ denote the geometric-mean
latencies of native execution, all-skips-off K050, projection-only K050,
and full K050. The gains from enabling projection and attention skipping
are $t_0/t_P$ and $t_P/t_K$, respectively. Both controls retain K050's fusion;
the former disables attention skipping in both implementations. These are
full-model latency ratios, not timings of the projection operations alone.
$t_N/t_0$ compares native execution with the optimized all-skips-off control,
which also changes dense kernels and layouts, so it does not isolate fusion.
Native-relative full speedup is $t_N/t_K$; $t_N$ uses the native measurements
paired with full K050. Each geometric mean uses 64 inputs, seven paired
passes, and three processes, matching the original timing protocol.

\paragraph{Execution units.}
QKV and FFN-up omit a $16\!\times\!8\!\times\!16$ MMA when the entire
$16$-token by $16$-feature activation fragment is zero. Their counters are
reconstructed from the actual BF16 operands using the implemented predicate.
FFN-down and attention output process eight real token rows per padded
16-row MMA. Rows with at most two nonzeros may use scalar operations under
the kernel's numerical safety checks; remaining empty feature fragments
bypass MMAs. These latter counters are instrumented and independently
checked against operand-derived counts. Their bypass totals include
replacement by scalar work as well as empty fragments, so they are not
counts of eliminated arithmetic. We pool bypassed and eligible MMA counts
across the four projection families before dividing, retaining the padding
and scalar-work distinctions in the supplementary data.

Attention omits an MMA when either complete operand fragment is zero.
In 29 checkpoints, QK skips $0\%$ and PV skips $10.42\%$ of eligible
instructions; the PV baseline includes masked or padded probability work.
Only seven-site + OL1 at $\kappa=0.5$ has additional skipping, reaching
$58.00\%$ for QK and $67.21\%$ for PV. Attention-skipping gain nevertheless
remains below one for every checkpoint ($0.9832$--$0.9894\times$).
These instruction counts include tiled causal padding, unlike the unmasked
scalar logical-product denominator.

\paragraph{Associations and matched recipes.}
Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-diagnostics} compares scalar and instruction-level
predictors. Projection-skipping gain has Pearson $r=0.973$, $R^2=0.946$
against projection MMA bypass, versus $r=0.643$, $R^2=0.413$ against the
projection scalar zero-product fraction. The within-family $\Smodel$ versus
native-relative speedup fits have $R^2=0.542$, $0.784$, and $0.762$ for the
baseline/one-site, four-site, and seven-site groups (ten checkpoints each).
Centering both variables within family gives $R^2=0.660$. The global relation
is therefore not solely separation between family means; all fits remain
descriptive and do not control for threshold, pressure, quality, or native cost.
Attention MMA skipping has only two distinct predictor values, with one
checkpoint at the larger value, limiting its correlation to that contrast.

Across all five matched OL1 thresholds, four-site recipes have greater
projection scalar contribution, more projection MMA bypass, and larger
projection-skipping gains than seven-site recipes. Without pressure, small
bypass differences change sign, although four-site has the larger gain at
each threshold. At $\kappa=0.5$ with OL1, four-site bypasses $82.30\%$ of
projection MMAs versus $57.68\%$ for seven-site. Its full K050 latency is
also lower: $0.45948$ versus $0.47337$ ms. Seven-site nevertheless reports
the larger native-relative speedup ($1.783\times$ versus $1.609\times$),
because its native baseline is slower ($0.84409$ versus $0.73944$ ms).
This relative-speedup/absolute-latency reversal holds in all eight
positive-threshold four-/seven-site pairs, with and without OL1.

The native implementation executes Q/K/V gates separately at nonzero
thresholds; K050 fuses them with layout conversion and RoPE, including in
the all-skips-off control. Similar all-skips-off latencies support native
gate overhead as an explanation for the denominator difference, but do
not isolate each operation's timing contribution.

\paragraph{Coverage and unavailable quantities.}
Logical sparsity uses retained FP16 counts. Kernel counters use actual BF16
operands over all 338 validation blocks; timing uses the original 64-input
subset. Pure-zero-only versus scalar-replacement bypass counts, counters
restricted to the timed inputs, unmasked-only attention MMA counts, and
isolated per-operation latency attribution are unavailable. No such values
are inferred from $\Smodel$. Supplementary \path{kernel/checkpoints.csv}
retains all 30 records with operation counts, skip counters, latencies,
and explicit missing fields; \path{kernel/associations.csv} and
\path{kernel/matched-pairs.csv} retain the complete fits and matched contrasts.
