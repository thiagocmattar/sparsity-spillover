# Observation 001: `R_model` versus full-model speedup

## Question

Does canonical `R_model` map consistently to measured sparse full-model
speedup across Pythia sizes and two GPU architectures?

## Method and coverage

The figure uses the verified Analysis 015 fresh-process reduction: 36 RTX PRO
4500 checkpoint deployments with three eager-only processes each and 18 H100
NVL endpoint sentinels with two eager-only processes each. Every primary
process uses 80 paired samples on 16 fixed inputs and complete 338-block
validation. Four known frozen-policy numerical failures remain visible and
are excluded from the fits. Integer zero-product and model-product counts are
rechecked before plotting.

## Legend / caption

Each point is a checkpoint-level median; vertical bars span independent
process estimates. Filled markers and solid lines denote RTX PRO 4500; open
markers and dashed lines denote H100 NVL. Marker shape identifies the trained
family. Red crosses fail the numerical gate. Lines are unweighted qualified
within-size OLS fits. Small horizontal offsets separate coincident GPU points
only.

## Result

The association is heterogeneous across both model size and hardware. A high
within-panel `R^2` does not imply useful acceleration: in particular, the six
H100 410M sentinels are positively ordered by `R_model` while every one remains
slower than native. Qualified `R^2` values are 0.347/0.0027/0.636 on RTX and
0.321/0.051/0.700 on H100 for 14M/70M/410M respectively.

## Caveats

These are descriptive one-seed checkpoint relations, not a scaling law or a
hardware-independent conversion from logical opportunity to runtime. H100
coverage is endpoint-only, and process ranges do not quantify training-seed
uncertainty.
