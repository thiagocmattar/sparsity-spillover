\begin{abstract}
Useful activation sparsity depends on how zeros are induced, which operations
they affect, and whether exploiting them repays execution overhead. We study
pressure, thresholding and site placement through 54 matched Pythia pretraining
conditions on MiniPile: detailed comparisons at 14M and selected complete
recipes at 70M and 410M. Model-wide activation sparsity, $\Smodel$, counts
zero-operand scalar multiplications in a declared workload; architectural
reach, $\Rarch$, describes the portion structurally affected by the selected
sites. Pressure's marginal quality--sparsity effect changes with the threshold
and target objective, from improving both observed axes to exchanging quality
for additional zeros. Distribution measurements and operation counts also show
that the seven-site recipe can produce greater model-wide sparsity despite
fewer pooled FFN zeros, because its attention contribution outweighs reduced
projection contributions. The high-threshold complete-recipe advantage
persists across the three sizes, but lower-threshold orderings vary. On one
RTX5090, a selected implementation achieves a $1.234\times$ geometric mean
speedup over native execution across the 30 14M checkpoints for BF16,
uncached 2,048-token inference. Sparse paths add $1.043\times$ over matched
fusion, helping 14 checkpoints; attention skipping slows every checkpoint.
These single-seed comparisons motivate evaluating an intervention's paired
quality cost, affected operations and execution granularity together.
\end{abstract}
