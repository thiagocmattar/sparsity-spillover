\begin{abstract}
Activation sparsity can accelerate language-model inference when specialized kernels skip computation associated with zero activations. Existing methods obtain sparsity through different combinations of training-time pressure ($P$), thresholding ($T$), and placement. We study these interventions under matched pretraining conditions across Pythia 14M and 70M models. We compare measures for model-wide activation sparsity, the architecture-dependent ceiling, and latency.
\textbf{Aggressive combinations of interventions extend the attainable sparsity range}: at $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $27.5\%$ and $40.6\%$ sparsity at 14M and 70M, respectively. These values represent $92\%$ and $82\%$ of the architectural ceilings, but validation loss increases by $0.62$ and $1.12$ over the corresponding base models.
\textbf{Sparsity and speedup increase together within recipes}, although not all sparsity translates into speedups; exploiting it requires specialized kernel development. The 70M model required a further optimization step because the 14M implementation did not transfer efficiently. For example, the sparser 14M recipe $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ achieves $27.48\%$ sparsity but is slower than $T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ at $12.71\%$ ($0.473$ versus $0.459$\,ms).
\textbf{Moderate thresholding with local FFN-hidden pressure ($T_4/P_h$) provides some of the best observed latency--quality trade-offs}, improving both metrics over broad pressure in all eight matched comparisons at $\kappa\leq0.1$. Local pressure also spills over: at 14M and $\kappa=0.05$, it increases sparsity at the unpressured attention-output site by $10.7$ percentage points relative to no pressure.
At 14M, the biggest latency gains come from bypassing the projections fed by FFN-hidden ($h$) and attention-output ($z$) activations: separate 14M ablations save $150$ and $29\,\mu$s per 2,048-token sequence on an RTX5090.
Training interventions and deployment kernels must be co-designed so that the induced sparsity is both high and structurally exploitable at inference time.
\end{abstract}
