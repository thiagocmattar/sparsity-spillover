# Observation 002: autoresearch speedup progress

## Question

How did the Run 025 closed-loop candidate trajectory change full-model speed
on a common sparse workload for each Pythia architecture?

## Method and coverage

For every candidate milestone with both measurements, the figure reports the
geometric mean of batch-one, sequence-2,048 full-model speedups at A4-OL1
`kappa=0.5` and A7-OL1 `kappa=0.5` on RTX PRO 4500. Vertical spans show the two
component endpoint values. Fixed numerical gates precede timing; later policy
stages use complete 338-block validation where recorded. Sample counts vary
from 48 to 160 per endpoint because the plot reconstructs the actual adaptive
trajectory rather than inventing a post-hoc matched rerun.

## Legend / caption

Circles are correctness-qualified paired-endpoint milestones, stars are frozen
final policies, and the red open diamond is K012: both plotted endpoints pass,
but the architecture policy was rejected after only three of six development
conditions passed complete validation. The dashed step is the best qualified
score observed so far. Candidate IDs without a connected point did not produce
a comparable two-endpoint score for that architecture.

## Result

Progress is architecture-specific and non-monotone. The 410M trajectory moves
from the slow but correct K004 tile path (0.5856x) to K010 (1.0147x). The 70M
trajectory crosses break-even only after topology/site specialization and ends
at 1.0213x. At 14M, K001 reaches 1.1382x versus 1.0461x for the later robust
frozen K013 policy, so the search gives back speed to satisfy broader
correctness.

## Caveats

The score uses two high-sparsity development endpoints and one GPU. It does not
summarize all checkpoints, validation loss, hardware transfer, or component
paths. Candidate measurements have unequal timing depth, and this single
adaptive trajectory cannot establish superiority over human or non-agentic
optimization.
