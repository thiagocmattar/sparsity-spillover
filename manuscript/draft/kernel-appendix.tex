\label{app:kernel-results}

\subsection{Execution mechanisms}
The specialized implementation preserves the trained weights and thresholding,
while fusing operations and exploiting zero activations. At $h,z$, \emph{sparse
execution} includes both skipping empty activation tiles and replacing matrix
instructions with scalar arithmetic for rows with very few nonzeros. The
FFN-down and attention-output projections share a fused kernel; the vocabulary
projection remains dense. Table~\ref{tab:kernel-paths} distinguishes work avoided
before and after operand loads.

\toprule
Operation & Execution rule & Work avoided \\
\midrule
QKV, FFN-up ($a,m$) & Check activation fragments after both operands are loaded.
& Skip matrix instructions for empty fragments; retain operand reads. \\

& Empty tiles avoid weight reads and matrix instructions. Scalar rows read

In Table~\ref{tab:kernel-mechanisms}, \emph{bypass} is omitted MMA instructions
divided by issued plus omitted instructions, pooling counts across layers and
all 338 validation sequences. Counts include padded work and, at $h,z$, matrix
instructions replaced by scalar arithmetic. Bypass therefore differs from
$\Smodel$, the model-wide fraction of zero-operand scalar products, and does not measure the
fraction of latency saved.

\subsection{Timing and numerical validation}
Measurements use an RTX\,5090, BF16, batch one, 2,048-token causal sequences,
and full vocabulary logits for every token (PyTorch 2.11.0, Transformers 5.12.1,
CUDA 12.8). This is full-sequence inference with fixed weights and shapes;
cached decoding and concurrent serving are outside the evaluated scope.
Final checkpoints were checked on all 338 validation sequences in three
independent processes. Relative to the unmodified eager reference, acceptance
required finite outputs, $|y-\hat y|\leq0.25+0.02|\hat y|$ for every logit,
relative $\ell_2$ error at most $0.02$ per sequence, and absolute mean
validation-loss difference at most $0.001$. Exact agreement in the 14M control
below is stronger than these general tolerances.

PyTorch/SDPA and specialized implementations use CUDA graphs with identical
inputs. We report geometric means of synchronized host timings over 64 fixed
validation sequences, seven randomized paired passes and three independent
processes. Timing includes embeddings, transformer blocks, final normalization,
vocabulary logits, thresholding and all input-dependent sparsity checks.
Compilation, static weight preparation, graph capture and equal input staging
are excluded. Activations are recomputed on every forward pass.

Cross-recipe speedups use one PyTorch/SDPA Base reference per model size and
measurement session: $t_{\mathrm{PyTorch},T_0/P_0}/t_{\mathrm{specialized},r}$.
Table~\ref{tab:endpoints-14m-70m} uses fixed native Base references of
0.65544\,ms at 14M and 1.61105\,ms at 70M. Figure~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency}
shows both native and specialized Base execution. The controls below instead
compare execution configurations of the same checkpoint, with their
denominators stated explicitly.

\subsection{14M execution controls}
\label{app:t2-hz-control}
Table~\ref{tab:kernel-mechanisms} disables individual sparse paths at fixed
weights, thresholds and fusion. Its ranges cover all off/on pairs of three
process means per mode; effects are conditional and non-additive. The common
14M implementation was not tuned separately for each recipe: it retained
attention skipping even though disabling it reduced latency in the historical
grid. These tests measure full-model effects, not separate memory-read,
inspection or arithmetic costs.

Table~\ref{tab:t2-hz-control} tests the narrower $T_2/P_h$ checkpoint at
$\kappa=0.1$. Disabling a site's sparse execution removes both empty-tile
skipping and scalar substitution, retaining the dense matrix path within the
same fused kernel. Thresholding and all other paths remain active.
Full-validation diagnostics confirm identical $h,z$ values and zero masks in
all six layers; logits agree exactly on all 338 sequences in three processes.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering\small
\caption{\textbf{14M $T_2/P_h$ execution control at $\kappa=0.1$.}
All configurations retain thresholding. Full-model geometric-mean latencies
follow the timing protocol above; sparse execution includes skipping and
scalar substitution.}
\label{tab:t2-hz-control}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccr@{}}
\toprule
Configuration & $h$ sparse execution & $z$ sparse execution & Latency (ms) \\
\midrule
A: full execution & On & On & 0.5588 \\
B: disable $h$ & Off & On & 0.6485 \\
C: disable $z$ & On & Off & 0.5630 \\
D: disable both & Off & Off & 0.6415 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Joint savings ($D-A$) are $82.7\,\mu$s, or $12.9\%$ of $D$'s latency
($12.8$--$13.0\%$ across processes). Disabling $h$ alone adds $89.7\,\mu$s.
Enabling $z$ saves $4.2\,\mu$s with $h$ on but adds $7.0\,\mu$s with $h$ off,
showing why the individual effects cannot be added. The original 14M
implementation, before adding independent execution switches, takes
0.5614\,ms ($12.5\%$ below $D$). Its $2.6\,\mu$s offset from $A$ matters when
interpreting the smaller $z$ effect.

PyTorch/SDPA on this same checkpoint takes 0.7018\,ms; $A$ reduces latency by
$20.4\%$, with $D-A$ accounting for $57.9\%$ of that gap. The remainder
includes fusion and other implementation differences. This share does not
apply to the historical comparison against the Base checkpoint.

\subsection{70M adaptation and execution control}
\label{app:kernel-transfer}
The initial 70M implementation retained the 14M execution rules; its best
sparse recipe reaches only $1.013\times$ the native Base speed. Replacing its
$h,z$ kernel with PyTorch operations removed almost the entire latency deficit
on the Base checkpoint, localizing the overhead to these projections. The dense
matrix path pads eight rows to sixteen and repeats activation inspection across
more output tiles at 70M. These are plausible sources of overhead; their
individual costs were not isolated.

We selected the optimized implementation on sixteen fixed training sequences
and fixed it before full-validation evaluation. It uses a row-sparsity prepass
for $h,z$, expands scalar execution to more sparse rows and changes the matrix
tiling. The $a,m$ projections use PyTorch,
attention uses a dense tiled kernel, and the vocabulary projection uses a tuned
CUTLASS kernel. Thus dense operations also contribute to the improvement.
Table~\ref{tab:70m-kernel-transfer} compares the implementations on three
representative checkpoints; Table~\ref{tab:endpoints-14m-70m} gives the full grid.

\input{tables/compact-results/70m-kernel-transfer}

At the lower-loss $T_2/P_h$, $\kappa=0.1$, optimization reduces latency from
2.258 to 1.956\,ms, still above the 1.662\,ms PyTorch reference on the same
checkpoint.

Table~\ref{tab:70m-hz-control} separates two controls on optimized 70M execution.
Disabling sparse $h,z$ execution removes skipping and scalar substitution,
retaining the dense matrix path within the same fused kernel. All modes pass
the full-validation numerical criteria above; bitwise agreement was not
established.

kernel's dense path. This benefit is conditional on that inefficient dense
reference. Against the faster native replacement, the reduction is $17.4\%$,
including fusion and layout differences.
