\subsection{Extensive ablations at 14M}

We first compare the broad $T_4$ and $T_7$ recipes at 14M, varying threshold strength and pressure placement (Figure~\ref{fig:14m-quality-latency}).
\textbf{Stronger interventions extend sparsity at a quality cost.}
At $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $27.48\%$ model-wide sparsity, or $91.8\%$ of its ceiling, while validation loss rises from the base model's $5.209$ to $5.829$.

\textbf{Local pressure preserves quality better than global pressure at moderate thresholds.}
For $\kappa\leq0.1$, $P_h$ lowers loss relative to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ in all eight matched $T_4/T_7$ comparisons, by $0.19$--$0.32$, while sparsity differs by at most $2.1$ percentage points. Local pressure also gives lower measured latency in all eight pairs.

\textbf{More sparsity does not necessarily imply lower latency.}
Within a fixed recipe, increasing sparsity generally reduces latency, but model-wide sparsity alone is not predictive. At $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ is substantially sparser than $T_4/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ ($27.48\%$ versus $12.71\%$), yet slightly slower (0.473 versus 0.459\,ms). This mismatch motivates examining where zeros occur and which sites contribute to latency savings.

\subsection{Spillover: Local pressure induces nonlocal sparsity}
\label{sec:paired-results}

\textbf{Local pressure produces a nonlocal response.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pressure-layer-sparsity} shows sparsity by site and layer under $P_0$, $P_h$ and $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ at 14M.
At $\kappa=0.05$, applying pressure only at $h$ increases $h$ sparsity from 74--85\% to 94--99\% for both $T_4$ and $T_7$.
The response, however, is not confined to the pressured site: sparsity at the unpressured $z$ activation rises from 71--85\% to 83--92\% under $T_4$, and from 70--87\% to 84--94\% under $T_7$, while changes at $a$ and $m$ are comparatively small.

In the other hand, broadening $T_7$ pressure to $P_{\mathrm{all}}$ makes both $h$ and $z$ less sparse than under $P_h$ in every layer. We speculate this happens because broad pressure reaches the OL1 norm cap much more often: $96.99\%$ of training steps, versus $0.25\%$ under $P_h$ across the threshold grid (Appendix~\ref{app:pressure-budget-diagnostics}). Competition for this budget may weaken pressure at $h$. Finally, at $\kappa=0.5$, $h$ and $z$ already exceed $99.5\%$ sparsity in every layer without pressure, whereas $T_7$'s $q,k,v$ remain at 13--36\%. Therefore, some sites may be more succeptible to sparsification than others.

\subsection{When sparsity translates to speedup}
\label{sec:activation-reshaping}
\label{sec:kernel-autoresearch}
% Controlled effects: Run037 observations/001-operation-latency.md;
% Analysis024/25_build_operation_latency_table.py; Run039 observations/001-am-port-verdict.md.

We next test whether the sites that respond most strongly also save execution time. Table~\ref{tab:kernel-mechanisms} isolates each skipping path at 14M by disabling it while keeping weights, gates and the other paths fixed. It reports \emph{bypass} (the percentage of omitted matrix instructions) and conditional saved time for $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ at $\kappa=0.5$.
\textbf{The measurable savings come from $h$ and $z$}: $150.1\,\mu$s and $29.0\,\mu$s, respectively.
The $a,m$ and joint $q,k$ effects remain unresolved against process variation, whereas $PV$ skipping adds $6.2\,\mu$s. These effects are conditional on the other paths remaining enabled and are not additive allocations of total savings.

Sparsity reduces latency when avoided work exceeds the overhead of detecting and skipping it.
At $h,z$, the sparse kernel checks $8\times16$ activation tiles
before loading their associated weights, so empty tiles avoid both weight reads and matrix multiplication. Applying the same strategy at $a$ and $m$ instead increased full-model latency by $17.2\%$ and $27.4\%$ on this checkpoint, with unchanged outputs.
Only $11.12\%$ of $a$'s $8\times16$ tiles were empty, and none at $m$, so
most groups still required matrix multiplication and the additional overhead was not justified.

Attention follows FlashAttention's tiled computation~\citep{dao2022flashattention}, but our zero checks occur after loading the operands. Skipping a matrix instruction therefore avoids arithmetic without avoiding those reads or the masking and softmax work. Softmax also turns zero scores into nonzero probabilities, so $q,k$ sparsity does not automatically propagate to $PV$.
Enabling $QK/PV$ skipping together raises
full-model latency from 0.514 to 0.521\,ms despite substantial bypass.
Thus, for this checkpoint and implementation, attention's arithmetic savings
do not offset its overhead, while $h/z$ benefit from avoiding weight reads
as well as computation.

\textbf{The sparsity and execution diagnostics converge on $h,z$.}
This motivates $T_2/P_h$: pressure remains local to $h$, while thresholding is restricted to $h,z$. At 14M and $\kappa=0.1$, it reaches $5.17\%$ model-wide sparsity against a $5.35\%$ ceiling, with loss $5.151$ versus Base's $5.209$, and latency 0.573\,ms versus native Base's 0.655\,ms (Figure~\ref{fig:14m-quality-latency}; Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-results}). The narrower recipe preserves quality while concentrating sparsity at the sites identified as profitable in the execution analysis.

% Hand-edited; Run048 observations/001-causal-hz-latency.md;
% results/hz-latency.json, generated by 07_reduce.py.
\textbf{Exploiting the induced zeros contributes directly to this operating point's latency reduction.}
In a separate controlled session on the same $T_2/P_h$ checkpoint at $\kappa=0.1$, disabling both $h,z$ skipping paths raises latency from 0.559 to 0.642\,ms: exploiting these zeros reduces full-model latency by \textbf{$12.9\%$}. Thresholding remains active, and all four execution configurations produce identical $h,z$ activations and validation logits. Disabling $h$ alone adds $89.7\,\mu$s, whereas disabling $z$ alone adds $4.2\,\mu$s. These conditional effects are not additive; the benefit at this operating point comes predominantly from $h$ (Appendix~\ref{app:t2-hz-control}).


\subsection{Results at 70M}
\label{sec:quality-sparsity-results}

We promote $T_2/P_h$ together with $T_4$ and $T_7$ under local or global pressure to 70M (Figure~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency}).
\textbf{$T_2/P_h$ preserves quality best among these three threshold topologies}, with the lowest validation loss at every matched $\kappa$. For $\kappa\leq0.1$, its loss is $4.129$--$4.193$ versus Base's $4.100$, at $14.63$--$15.21\%$ sparsity against a $15.44\%$ ceiling. Broader interventions extend sparsity further at greater quality cost: $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $40.60\%$ at $\kappa=0.5$, or $82.2\%$ of its ceiling, with loss $5.216$ ($1.12$ above Base).

\textbf{The 14M kernel does not transfer efficiently to 70M.}
Although latency decreases as sparsity increases within recipes, implementation overhead leaves the lower-loss recipes slower than native PyTorch. We therefore perform an additional optimization step, improving both sparse $h,z$ execution and dense components. This reduces $T_7/P_h$ latency at $\kappa=0.5$ from 1.590 to 1.088\,ms. The resulting $1.481\times$ speedup over native Base is an \textbf{end-to-end comparison}: it includes both a different checkpoint and sparse and dense implementation changes. All 27 checkpoints pass full-validation numerical checks in three fresh processes (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-transfer}).

% Controlled 70M evidence: Analysis028 observations/001-matched-70m-grid.md;
% 04_controls.py and data/retained-controls.json, from Run042 (separate session).
\textbf{The specialized $h,z$ path provides an additional execution benefit at 70M.}
In a separate controlled session, the optimized $T_7/P_h$ checkpoint at $\kappa=0.5$ takes 1.128\,ms. Replacing only its specialized $h,z$ implementation with native dense operations, while retaining the same weights, thresholds and other optimized components, raises latency to 1.365\,ms. The specialized path therefore reduces full-model latency by \textbf{$17.4\%$} relative to this control. This conditional benefit includes fusion, layout and inspection costs alongside sparsity exploitation. Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-transfer} also reports the control that disables sparse $h,z$ execution within the same optimized implementation.

The lower-loss $T_2/P_h$ points at $\kappa\leq0.1$, however, still take 1.956--2.504\,ms after optimization, slower than the 1.611\,ms native Base. At $\kappa=0.5$, latency falls to 1.214\,ms ($1.364\times$ its same-session native Base at 1.656\,ms), but loss rises to 4.838. The quality ordering therefore transfers to 70M, while the favorable 14M quality--latency balance does not transfer directly.
