\section{Experimental study}
\label{sec:experiments}

We pretrain Pythia-14M, 70M, and 410M from random initialization~\citep{biderman2023pythia} on MiniPile~\citep{kaddour2023minipile}. Each run receives 712 optimizer steps and 1.493 billion input tokens. Within each size, runs share initialization, data order and training budget. We evaluate final-checkpoint validation loss and $\Smodel$ on the full validation set. Table~\ref{tab:interventions} lists the intervention recipes; Appendix~\ref{app:training-protocol} gives the full protocol.

\textbf{Roles of the three model sizes.}
The 14M study provides controlled intervention comparisons; 70M tests whether the observed regimes recur at a larger size. We retain 410M as a fixed-token sparsity stress test, rather than a quality-scaling result. Its base-model loss exceeds 70M's under a smaller token-to-parameter ratio and a lower learning rate; these effects are not isolated. Appendix~\ref{app:410m-results} reports all 410M endpoints and clipping curves, and Figure~\ref{fig:a0-optimization} shows the training trajectories. The detailed intervention and runtime analyses therefore focus on 14M and 70M.

For comparison, we apply TEAL-style post-hoc magnitude thresholding~\citep{liu2024teal} at $a,m,h,z$, using percentile targets $p=0,0.1,\ldots,0.9$. Site--layer thresholds are calibrated separately on ten training blocks. Figures~\ref{fig:quality-sparsity-overview} and~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency} show the quality--sparsity trade-offs and full-model latencies at 14M and 70M, respectively.

\textbf{Interventions extend sparsity at a quality cost.}
At $\kappa=0.5$, $T_7/P_{\mathrm{all}}$ reaches $27.48\%$ and $40.60\%$ model-wide sparsity at 14M and 70M: $91.8\%$ and $82.2\%$ of the respective ceilings. Validation loss rises by $0.62$ and $1.12$ relative to each size's base model. At moderate thresholds ($\kappa\leq0.1$), pressure confined to $h$ gives lower loss than global pressure at similar sparsity; Section~\ref{sec:paired-results} examines the matched comparisons.

\textbf{Kernel efficiency does not transfer automatically.}
The original 70M port takes 3.313\,ms on the base model versus 1.664\,ms for native PyTorch; even its best sparse checkpoint reaches only $1.028\times$ the native base-model speed. Controlled component replacements locate most overhead at $h,z$: their implementation repeats sparsity checks and pads eight real rows to sixteen-row matrix instructions, making dense computation expensive at 70M. A separate optimization retained sparse $h,z$ execution with wider output tiles and restored native $a,m$ projections and attention. For $T_7/P_h$ at $\kappa=0.5$, it reached 1.329\,ms versus a same-session native base of 1.589\,ms ($1.195\times$), with unchanged weights and thresholds. This two-checkpoint follow-up does not replace the grid in Figure~\ref{fig:70m-quality-latency} or establish a scale-dependent speedup trend. Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-transfer} gives the diagnosis, numerical checks and scope.
