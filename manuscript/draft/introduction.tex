\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Activation sparsity can reduce inference cost when specialized kernels exploit
zero activations~\citep{liu2024teal,cetin2026sparser}. Choosing how to create
those zeros requires knowing which operations their sites affect and whether
skipping that work repays its overhead. We ask how pressure, thresholding
and placement change sparsification's quality cost and its useful computation.

Existing recipes combine these choices differently: TEAL uses post-hoc
thresholding, ProSparse combines ReLU with progressive pressure, and
Q-Sparse selects projection inputs~\citep{liu2024teal,song2024prosparse,wang2024qsparse}.
We distinguish \emph{pressure}, which encourages small magnitudes,
\emph{thresholding}, which applies a zeroing map, and \emph{placement},
which selects where each acts. Fixed cutoffs let us examine their interactions
while training determines how many activations survive.

We pretrain Pythia-family models from scratch for controlled comparisons
across sizes~\citep{biderman2023pythia}. At 14M, 30 conditions and 29 paired
contrasts show pressure's threshold-dependent effect: at $\kappa=0.5$,
adding OL1 to seven-site thresholding raises model-wide sparsity from
$15.39\%$ to $27.48\%$ for $+0.1265$ loss. Architectural reach, computed
before training from the graph and workload, puts this result against
$29.95\%$ structurally reachable work. A7-OL1 also improves over A4-OL1
by 14.77 sparsity pp and 0.2086 loss, despite fewer pooled FFN zeros.
Selected recipes extend to 70M and 410M, where that high-threshold comparison
again improves both axes, adding 5.01 and 9.02 pp; lower-threshold orderings
vary. ReLU pretraining also improves the response to subsequent clipping
at intermediate targets across all sizes, while retaining an initial quality
cost and extreme-target reversals.

An execution case study tests which counted opportunities become useful.
For BF16, batch-one, uncached 2,048-token inference on an RTX5090, the
selected implementation gains $1.234\times$ geometrically over native execution across
30 checkpoints, but sparse paths add only $1.043\times$ over matched fusion.
Disabling attention skipping improves every checkpoint. These controls connect
intervention choices to the granularity and overhead that determine latency.
